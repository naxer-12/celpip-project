By Matthew Zare, CELPIP teacher at CELTESTPIPChecked October 21, 2026 against IRCC's CRS criteria and its own calculator

Your CRS score is the number IRCC uses to rank you in the Express Entry pool. From November 3, 2026 it comes from five parts: core factors (your age, education and official languages, up to 420 points), Canadian work or a job offer (IRCC's new labour market integration factor, up to 150), your spouse or partner's factors (up to 40), skill transferability (up to 100) and additional points such as a provincial nomination (up to 600).

The calculator above follows IRCC's published criteria. Before publishing it, we ran 60 sample profiles through IRCC's own tool and every line of every result matched. The new high-wage and job offer points follow the tables IRCC published on October 16, 2026. Your official score is still the one IRCC shows in your Express Entry profile.

How is the CRS score calculated?

Five parts, added together. Each part has its own ceiling, and some of them change when a spouse or partner comes with you to Canada.

"With a spouse or partner" only applies when they are coming to Canada with you and are not already a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. Otherwise you are scored as single, even if you are married. Until November 2, 2026, Canadian work sits inside the core factors (up to 80, or 70 with a spouse or partner) and the whole score tops out at 1,200. What changes on November 3, 2026?

Canadian work and job offers move into one new factor, and from November 3, both can earn more. IRCC calls it the labour market integration factor. You get the higher of two options, never both:

  • Canadian work: the same points as before for your years of skilled work in Canada (40 to 80, or 35 to 70 with a spouse or partner), plus 20, 40 or 70 more if at least 12 months of it were in a high-wage occupation.
  • A job offer: 50, 70 or 100 points for a qualifying offer in a high-wage occupation, the same with or without a spouse. No labour market impact assessment (LMIA) is needed.

A high-wage occupation is a NOC whose median wage, across everyone in Canada doing that job, is at least 1.3 times the average median wage of the whole workforce. Your own salary does not count. Check your occupation in the list of all 186.

For example, a single applicant with 3 years in Canada as a software engineer (NOC 21231, 1.5×) gets 64 + 40 = 104 for this factor, up from 64. A 2× job offer on top would not add anything: it is worth 100, and the higher of the two (104) counts. Someone with no Canadian work and a 1.5× offer gets 70, up from 0.

Skill transferability does not change: it still counts Canadian work by years only. Everyone already in the pool is rescored automatically. IRCC says the recalculation can take up to two weeks and pauses invitation rounds while it runs, and asks anyone with a job offer that has no LMIA to add it to their profile before November 3, 2026. Sources: IRCC, 2026 changes and backgrounder.

How many CRS points does language give you?

Language is the biggest part of your score that you can still change. Each of the four skills (listening, reading, writing, speaking) earns points on its own, so one weak skill costs you points even when the other three are strong.

The jumps that matter most are CLB 7 and CLB 9. Going from CLB 6 to CLB 7 nearly doubles a skill's points, and CLB 9 in all four skills also unlocks the top tier of skill transferability. Your test result has to be converted to a CLB level first: a CELPIP level counts as the same CLB level from CLB 4 up, where IRCC's tables start, while IELTS, PTE Core, TEF and TCF each have their own table. Our CLB calculator converts all five tests skill by skill. IRCC accepts CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training and PTE Core for English and TEF Canada and TCF Canada for French, with results under two years old (IRCC, language test results).

How much is Canadian work experience worth?

Up to 80 points for five years (70 with a spouse or partner), and up to 150 (140) from November 3, 2026 if your work was in a high-wage occupation. Work counts when it was skilled (a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 job: the NOC code finder shows yours), paid, and full-time or the same total hours part-time, within the last 10 years, while you had temporary resident status, for a Canadian employer while you were in Canada. Remote work counts if you were physically in Canada (IRCC, CRS criteria). Self-employment, work while you were studying full-time and unauthorized work do not count, except that some physicians can count self-employed time providing publicly funded medical services.

Which additional points can you get?

Four things, capped at 600 together:

  • A provincial or territorial nomination: 600 points, which on its own usually means an invitation.
  • French: 25 points for NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills, or 50 if you also have CLB 5 or higher in all four English skills.
  • Study in Canada: 15 or 30 points, as above.
  • A brother or sister living in Canada: 15 points, if they are 18 or older and a citizen or permanent resident. Your accompanying spouse's sibling counts too.

Job offers are no longer part of the additional points. They earned nothing from March 25, 2025, and from November 3, 2026 an offer in a high-wage occupation counts in the new Canadian work or job offer factor instead.

How does a spouse or common-law partner change your score?

Your own ceilings drop a little, and your partner can make up the difference. When a spouse or partner comes with you and is not a citizen or permanent resident, your core factors are worth up to 390 instead of 420, and their own education, language and Canadian work can add up to 40.

What CRS score do you need for an invitation?

There is no fixed pass mark. IRCC holds rounds of invitations and sets the lowest score invited in each one. Cut-offs depend on how many candidates are in the pool, how many invitations the round offers and the type of round: general rounds, rounds for one program such as the Canadian Experience Class or provincial nominees, and category rounds such as French-language proficiency. IRCC publishes every past round on its rounds of invitations page.

How can you raise your CRS score?

Start with language. It is the largest part of the score you control:

  • Language: for a single applicant, moving a skill from CLB 7 to CLB 9 adds 14 points, so 56 across all four skills, before any skill transferability points it also unlocks.
  • French: NCLC 7 in all four French skills as a second language adds 12 second-language points and 50 additional points if your English is CLB 5 or higher.
  • Canadian work: your first year in Canada is worth 40 points for a single applicant, plus transferability points, and from November 3, 2026 up to 70 more if it was in a high-wage occupation.
  • Education: a master's instead of a bachelor's adds 15 core points, and can move you up a transferability tier.
  • A provincial nomination adds 600.

The "Ways to add points" list in the calculator works these out for your own answers, caps included. If CELPIP is your English test, scored practice sets for every part show which skill is holding your CLB down.

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum CRS score?

1,200 points until November 2, 2026. From November 3, 2026, the maximums IRCC has published add up to 1,270: core factors 420 (390 with an accompanying spouse or partner), Canadian work or a job offer 150 (140), spouse factors 40, skill transferability 100 and additional points 600. IRCC has not yet published the amended Ministerial Instructions that state the new total.

Do job offers give CRS points again?

Yes, from November 3, 2026, if the job is in a high-wage occupation. A qualifying offer is worth 50, 70 or 100 points by the occupation's level, and no LMIA is needed. It is not added to your Canadian work points: the higher of the two counts. From March 25, 2025 to November 2, 2026, job offers added nothing.

What is the labour market integration factor?

IRCC's new CRS factor for Canadian work and job offers, from November 3, 2026. It gives you the higher of your Canadian work points (your years, plus 20 to 70 for 12 months in a high-wage occupation) or your job offer points (50 to 100), up to 150, or 140 with an accompanying spouse or partner.

Does this calculator give the same result as IRCC's tool?

Yes for every rule IRCC's tool uses today. On October 13, 2026 we ran 60 sample profiles through IRCC's own Check your score tool and every line of every result matched. The high-wage and job offer points follow the tables IRCC published on October 16, 2026 for November 3. As of October 21, 2026, IRCC's tool still scores the rules in force today, without those points, so with Canadian work in a high-wage occupation or a qualifying job offer it shows a lower total than ours. Your official score is the one IRCC shows in your Express Entry profile.

Which language tests count for Express Entry?

Five tests. CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training and PTE Core for English, and TEF Canada and TCF Canada for French. Your results must be less than two years old when you create your profile and when you apply.

Does my spouse's language score count?

Only if they come with you and are not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. Their first official language is worth up to 5 points per skill, 20 in all, as part of the 40 spouse points.

Is a higher CELPIP level worth retaking the test for?

Often, yes. For a single applicant, CLB 9 instead of CLB 7 adds 14 points per skill, 56 across four skills, and CLB 9 in all four can add up to 50 more through skill transferability.

Check your CELPIP level before you book

Your CRS score moves most with language, and language is the part you can train. Answer a scored practice set in every CELPIP part to see which skill is holding you below CLB 9. If you already have scores from another test, the CLB calculator shows the level and points for each skill, and the high-wage occupations list shows what your job is worth from November 3, 2026.

IRCC's own calculator is on its Check your score page.
