What is a CELPIP practice test?

A CELPIP practice test is a timed set of real-format questions from one or more of the 20 task types on the CELPIP-General test. On this page you practice a single task type at a time, with a clock and an answer key, rather than sitting all four sections at once.

What is the difference between a CELPIP practice test and a CELPIP mock test?

A practice test covers one task type under its own clock, usually 9 to 27 minutes. A mock test covers all four sections in a single sitting of about three hours. Use practice tests to fix a specific weakness and mock tests to check your stamina and timing. Mock exams are on the exams page.

05

How many CELPIP practice questions should I do?

Mistakes understood matter more than questions attempted. One timed task type a day, followed by ten minutes reviewing why you missed what you missed, beats rushing through large volumes without review.

Can I practice CELPIP Writing and Speaking online and get a score?

Yes. Writing and Speaking responses are scored automatically and returned with a band plus a breakdown across Content and Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability, and Task Fulfillment.

09

What CELPIP score do I need for Express Entry?

It depends on the program, and the minimum applies to each of the four abilities, according to IRCC. The Federal Skilled Worker Program needs CLB 7 in all four. The Canadian Experience Class needs CLB 7 for TEER 0 or 1 jobs and CLB 5 for TEER 2 or 3 jobs. The Federal Skilled Trades Program needs CLB 5 in Speaking and Listening and CLB 4 in Reading and Writing. Aim higher than the floor if you want a competitive profile, because the CRS points move sharply above CLB 9.

How long are CELPIP results valid?

Less than two years old when you complete your Express Entry profile and when you submit your application for permanent residence, per IRCC.

What is a CELPIP practice test, and how is it different from a full mock exam?

A CELPIP practice test is one task type at a time. A mock exam is all four sections back to back under a single clock. Both matter, and most people need them in that order.

You practice task types to fix a specific weakness. If you keep losing marks on Reading Part 4, you do twenty Part 4 sets in a row until the pattern stops surprising you. You sit a mock exam to find out whether your stamina holds and whether your timing survives contact with the real thing. Doing mock exams first is the most common mistake I see. People burn through their full tests early, score badly across the board, and learn nothing specific enough to act on.

Where should you start if you have never taken CELPIP?

Want to practise on paper first? Download our free CELPIP practice test with answers PDF: a full Writing and Speaking section in the real order and timing, a sample answer for all 10 tasks, and links to free online Listening and Reading sets.

Start with Reading Part 1 and Listening Part 1, because those two tell you the most about your baseline in the least time. Reading Part 1 gives you 11 questions in 11 minutes. Listening Part 1 gives you three short audio segments that play once and never again.

Do one of each before you read a single strategy article. You need a number to work against, and you cannot get one from theory. Once you have it, the routing is straightforward:

  • You ran out of time on Reading Part 1. Your problem is scanning speed, not vocabulary. Work through Reading tips and strategies and then repeat Part 1 with a visible timer.
  • You understood the Listening audio but forgot it by the time you answered. Your problem is note-taking. Start with Listening Part 3, which is the part that rewards a two-column note layout more than any other.
  • You froze on Writing or Speaking. Your problem is structure, not English. Go to the Writing Task 1 email template or the Speaking task templates first, then come back and practice.
  • You did fine and want to know what "fine" is worth. Check the CELPIP to CLB conversion chart.

If you are not yet sure how the four sections fit together, the test format guide lays out all four in one table.

What does a CELPIP practice question in the real test format actually look like?

Here is a real one, taken from the free Reading Part 1 set on this site. The passage is an email from Derek to Kevin about a borrowed camping trailer. Partway through, Derek explains that a technician at an Ottawa repair shop found the sealant around the trailer's window frame had dried out and cracked, and that the same technician separately fixed a slow leak in one of the tire rims.

Question 3. The technician found that the roof leak was caused by .....

  • a) a crack in the rear window glass
  • b) deteriorated sealant around the window frame ✓
  • c) a hole in the trailer's roof panel
  • d) water pooling from a damaged tire rim

The answer is (b), and the two wrong answers that catch people are worth more than the right one.

Option (a) swaps the window frame for the window glass. If you scanned for the word "window" and stopped, you picked it. Option (d) reuses the tire rim, which the passage does mention, just as a separate and unrelated repair. Both distractors are built from real words in the text placed in the wrong relationship to each other.

That is the whole design of Reading Part 1. It rewards you for matching the paraphrase and punishes you for spotting the keyword. "Deteriorated sealant around the window frame" never appears in the passage in those words. What appears is "the sealant around the window frame had dried out and cracked", and you have to accept that those are the same fact.

Work the free set yourself and check the other ten: Reading Part 1: Correspondence. If you want the reasoning behind the harder question types across all four sections, this breakdown of the hardest questions goes deeper than the study guide does.

How many practice questions do you need before test day?

Fewer than you think, done properly. Twenty questions you review carefully will move your score further than two hundred you rush through and never look at again.

The number that matters is not questions attempted, it is mistakes understood. A useful rhythm for most people: one timed task type per day, then ten minutes reading the answer key and writing down why you missed what you missed. If your reason is always "careless", you have not looked hard enough yet. Careless is not a reason, it is a category of reasons.

How long you need depends on where you are starting and how far you have to go. If your test is close, the last minute study plan is built for compressed timelines. If you have a month or more, the four week plan for beginners paces it properly. Either way, read the mistakes that cost the most marks early rather than late, because most of them are habits, and habits take repetitions to unlearn.

How do you practice CELPIP Writing and Speaking without a teacher?

You submit a response and get it scored, which is the part self-study normally cannot give you. Reading and Listening have answer keys. Writing and Speaking do not, and that is where most people plateau, because you cannot mark your own register mismatch or spot your own missing content point.

CELPIP Writing is scored on Content and Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability, and Task Fulfillment. That last one is the one people lose marks on without realising it. Writing Task 1 asks for about 150 to 200 words and gives you three explicit bullet points to cover. Miss one of the three, and no amount of good English rescues the score, because you did not do the task that was set.

On this site, Writing and Speaking submissions are scored automatically and come back with a band and a breakdown rather than a single number. AI scoring requires a login, and Writing and Speaking submissions run on a free credit allowance before Pro. Each Writing prompt also carries three sample responses at different levels plus the task template and a vocabulary list, so you can compare your answer against a model before you submit it.

For complete Writing sample answers explained level by level, see our CELPIP Writing Task 1 samples with answers.

Before you write anything, read the structure guides: Writing Task 1, the email and Writing Task 2, the survey response. Both tasks reward a predictable shape, and the 53 minutes you get for the Writing section (27 for Task 1, 26 for Task 2, per the format guide) does not leave room to invent a structure on the day.

Are free CELPIP practice test PDFs worth using?

They are worth something for content and close to nothing for conditions. A PDF can show you what the question types look like. It cannot reproduce a computer-delivered test, which is the thing that actually catches people out.

CELPIP is fully computer-delivered. You read on screen, you type your emails, you speak into a headset against a visible countdown, and in Listening the audio plays once with no replay button. None of that survives being printed. People who prepare entirely on paper walk in fluent and still lose marks to the interface, because they have never practiced against a clock they cannot pause.

So use a PDF for the sections that work on paper, and the screen for the rest. Our free CELPIP practice test with answers PDF does exactly that: it gives you a full Writing section and all 8 Speaking tasks with the real time limits and a sample answer for each, and it sends you to free online sets for Listening and Reading, where the audio plays once and the clock runs like it will on test day. For Reading on paper, there is also our Reading practice test PDF with all 38 questions and an answer key. Paragon Testing, which makes the test, also publishes free official practice material. Learn the format on paper, then do your timed repetitions on screen, because that is how you will sit the real test.

What CELPIP score do you need, and how much practice gets you there?

It depends on the program you are applying to, and the two most common targets are far apart.

Your goalWhat you needSource
Express Entry (FSW, FSTP, CEC), first official languageCLB 7 in all four abilities, minimumIRCC
Canadian citizenship, ages 18 to 54CLB 4 in Speaking and Listening onlyIRCC
A competitive Express Entry profileCLB 9 and above, where the CRS points jumpIRCC

CLB means Canadian Language Benchmark, the 1 to 12 scale Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) uses to read your test result. Your CELPIP results must be less than two years old when you complete your Express Entry profile and again when you submit your application for permanent residence.

Aim above the floor. CLB 7 is a minimum, not a target, and the difference between scraping CLB 7 and holding CLB 9 is worth a large number of CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) points. For the full mapping, the per-program minimums and how the points actually move, see the CELPIP scores and immigration guide.
