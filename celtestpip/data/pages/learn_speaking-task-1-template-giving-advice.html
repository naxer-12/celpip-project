The 1-3-1 template

  1. Greeting

    Hi [Name],

  2. Context

    Congratulations on the [News]! It is so exciting to hear that...

  3. Why me

    I can definitely give you some advice because I have actually successfully [Activity] myself recently.

  4. Advice 1

    First and foremost, if you want to fix this, you absolutely must [Action].

  5. Advice 2

    Another step you ought to take is to [Action].

  6. Advice 3

    One final thing is, if I were in your shoes, I would definitely [Action].

  7. Close

    I know you will make the right choice. Good luck!

CELPIP Speaking Task 1 Template (Giving Advice) + Full Answer

The full CELPIP Speaking Course is free: CELPIP Speaking Course · More lessons on the CELTESTPIP YouTube channel

Goal

Help someone either make a decision or prepare for something (that is how CELPIP itself describes Task 1). Speak to that person directly, give practical suggestions with a reason for each, and keep your tone helpful and thoughtful.

The 90 seconds sound generous until the timer starts. Most people who lose marks here are not short of English. They are short of a plan, so they spend the first twenty seconds circling the situation and then rush three half-finished suggestions into the last thirty. The template below exists to stop that.

How long is CELPIP Speaking Task 1?

Task 1 gives you 30 seconds to prepare and 90 seconds to speak. CELPIP publishes those times in its 2026 Speaking Pro study pack.

Ninety seconds makes Task 1 one of the two longest responses in the whole Speaking section. Only Task 7 matches it. That matters for how you rehearse, because a script that fits comfortably into 60 seconds will leave you standing in silence for the last half-minute, and silence is expensive on a test that scores how easy you are to listen to.

Is Giving Advice Task 1 or Task 2?

Giving Advice is Task 1. CELPIP's Guidebook for Test Takers lists eight Speaking tasks, and Giving Advice is the first of them.

On screen, the task is titled "Speaking Task 1: Giving Advice". That is the label in CELPIP's own screenshots in its 2026 study pack. If a forum or a prep book calls it "task 2" or "part 2", the label is theirs, not CELPIP's, and you are on the right page.

What the examiner scores on Task 1: the four CELPIP criteria

CELPIP Speaking is rated on four dimensions. Grammar is not a separate one: CELPIP lists it inside Listenability.

01Content/Coherence

The question behind it

Are your ideas well organised and fully developed?

02Vocabulary

The question behind it

Do you have enough words, and do you use them naturally and accurately?

03Listenability

The question behind it

Can a listener follow you without effort?

04Task Fulfillment

The question behind it

Did you do what the task asked, in a tone that suits the listener?

Read that list twice if you have been studying from IELTS material. There is no standalone Grammar band and no "Coherence and Cohesion" on this test. Those are IELTS terms, and a surprising number of CELPIP blogs repeat them.

Grammar still matters. CELPIP puts grammar, and how much you vary your sentences, under Listenability, which is why the grammar work in this template is built around range and clarity rather than around avoiding every error. A rater who has to work hard to follow you is marking Listenability down whether or not your tenses are perfect.

Task Fulfillment is the one people lose without noticing. The prompt asks you to advise a specific person about a specific thing. General observations about the topic do not fulfil the task. Advice does.

What do raters look at under each dimension?

Here is what each dimension covers, in our words, and what it means on Task 1:

DimensionWhat raters look at (our summary)What it means on Task 1
Content/CoherenceEnough good ideas, in a clear order, each backed by an example or a detailAt least three suggestions, each with a reason or an example, in a clear order
VocabularyThe right word for the job, used naturally, with some range and precisionPrecise advice: "send an agenda the day before", not "prepare well"
ListenabilityNatural pace and pronunciation, pauses that do not break the flow, clear grammar and varied sentencesMix "must", "ought to" and "if I were you", and keep a steady pace
Task FulfillmentStays on topic, covers the whole prompt, suits the listener, uses the timeAdvise this person about this problem, sound helpful, and use most of the 90 seconds

Our summary, based on CELPIP's Speaking Performance Standards in the Speaking Pro study pack (2026).

If you want to see how those four dimensions turn into a number, the CELPIP Speaking score chart maps CELPIP levels onto CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) bands.

What does a CELPIP Speaking Task 1 question look like?

You get one short situation about a person who needs help, a 30 second preparation timer, and then 90 seconds of recording. Here are two prompts in that shape, written by us:

Your cousin has just accepted a job in a city where she does not know anyone. She is worried she will feel lonely after the move. Give her advice on how to settle in and meet new people.

Your friend wants to grow vegetables on his apartment balcony this summer, but he has never grown anything before. Give him advice on how to get started.

Our example: a Level 8 style answer vs a Level 10 style answer

Take the first prompt, the cousin who is moving. Here is how two answers to it can differ. These are CELTESTPIP's own examples, written to show the difference. CELPIP did not rate them.

Level 8 style answer (our example)Level 10 style answer (our example)
IdeasThree ideas: join a club, talk to co-workers, call home oftenThe same three ideas
SupportOne short line each: "You should join a club. It is good to meet people."A reason and a detail for each: "Look for something that meets every week, like a Tuesday run club, because seeing the same faces every week is how strangers turn into friends."
Moving between ideas"First", "also", "and""Another thing you could do is...", "If I were you, I would..."
Word choiceGeneral: "do activities", "it is nice"Precise: "a weekly volunteer shift", "a standing Sunday call with your parents"
Talking to herDrifts into "people should..."Uses her name and keeps saying "you"

Both answers give three suggestions. The Level 10 style one is not using rarer words. It develops each idea and makes it specific, and that is the step up this template is built to give you.

What does CELPIP itself say to do on Task 1?

CELPIP's study pack gives five strategies for this task. Here they are in our words, with where each one sits in the template.

CELPIP's strategy, in our wordsWhere it is in the template
Talk to the person, as if they are sitting in front of you.Step 1: greet them by name, then keep saying "you", never "he" or "she"
Give three or more pieces of advice, not one or two.Step 2: Advice #1, #2 and #3
Back up every piece of advice with a reason or a real-life example.The two supports under each piece of advice
Use a linking phrase each time you move to a new piece of advice, so the listener hears the change.The openers in Step 2: "Another step you ought to take is...", "One final thing is, if I were in your shoes..."
Sound kind and helpful, like someone who wants things to go well for them.The "why me" line in Step 1 and the short, warm close in Step 3

Look at the fourth strategy. The transitions CELPIP suggests are the ones this template has used all along, so this is not a trick raters dislike. It is the shape CELPIP itself recommends. What raters do not want is the same words for every prompt, which is why the next sections give you a frame and not a script.

Step 1: The Opening

Goal: Greet the person, establish context, and validate your expertise (explain why you are the right person to ask).

Grammar Booster: Present Perfect Tense

To prove you are qualified to give advice, use the Present Perfect structure. This shows your experience started in the past and continues to be relevant now.

Formula

Subject + Have + Past Participle

Keywords

have learned, have experienced, have done

Structure

  1. 1Greeting

    Hi [Name].

  2. 2Context

    Acknowledge the situation (Congrats, thanks, etc.).

  3. 3Validation

    Explain your experience.

Keep this to about fifteen seconds. It buys you credibility cheaply, and every second past fifteen is a second stolen from your advice.

Step 2: The Main Advice (The "3-Point Strategy")

Goal: Give 3 distinct pieces of advice using different grammar structures for variety.

Grammar: The Hierarchy of Modals

You must show a "range of intensity." Do not use "should" three times. Use the table below to select your vocabulary.

IntensityModalsUsageExample
High (Strong)Must / Have toUrgent, critical advice. If they don't do this, they fail."You must arrive early."
Medium (Standard)Should / Ought toGood, standard advice. The right thing to do."You ought to wear a suit."
Low (Soft)Could / MightSuggestions, options, or polite possibilities."You might consider taking a taxi."
HypotheticalWouldUsed in 2nd Conditionals (imaginary situations)."I would choose the red one."

That range is doing direct work on your Vocabulary score, which CELPIP describes as whether you can use a range of words naturally. Three "shoulds" in ninety seconds is a narrow range, and it sounds like one.

Advice #1: The Strongest Point

Use this for your most urgent advice.

Grammar

1st Conditional + Strong Modal

Rule

If + Present Tense (Action) ... Subject + Must (Result)

Structure

  1. 1The Advice

    "If you want to stop people from scrolling past you, you must focus on the first three seconds."

  2. 2Support 1 (Reason)

    "This is known as the 'hook' and it is the only time you have to grab their attention."

  3. 3Support 2 (Consequence)

    "Since people scroll very fast, if you don't catch them immediately, they will just swipe away."

Advice #2: The Practical Point

Use this for standard, helpful advice.

Grammar

Medium Modals

Rule

Subject + Ought to + Base Verb (Use Ought to instead of "Should" to score higher).

Structure

  1. 1The Advice

    "Another thing you could do is check your lighting. You ought to film facing a window."

  2. 2Support 1 (Detail)

    "You should always prioritize natural daylight over artificial lamps."

  3. 3Support 2 (Benefit)

    "This is important because dark videos often get ignored by viewers, regardless of how good the content is."

Advice #3: The Hypothetical Point

Use this to offer a suggestion by putting yourself in their shoes.

Grammar

2nd Conditional + Modal

Rule

If I were you ... I would + Base Verb

Note

Note: Always use "were", never "was".

Structure

  1. 1The Advice

    "One final thing is, If I were you, I would use trending music in the background."

  2. 2Support 1 (Context)

    "Algorithms often push videos that use popular songs significantly more than others."

  3. 3Support 2 (Outcome)

    "This implies that simply adding a song could get your video in front of thousands of new people."

At least three suggestions is CELPIP's own advice. Two supports under each one is our teaching choice, because it reliably fills ninety seconds without padding. If you are a fast speaker, add a third support to your strongest point rather than inventing a fourth piece of advice.

Step 3: The Closing

Goal: A quick, friendly sign-off.

Structure

Rule

Keep it short. Do not summarize the advice.

Example

"I can't wait to see what you create. Good luck with your first post!"

Sample question 1: A nervous first-time meeting lead

Prompt

A colleague has been asked to run their first team meeting next week and is nervous about it. Give them advice on how to prepare.

Model answer (about 90 seconds)

Hi Daniel, thanks for telling me about the meeting. It is completely normal to feel nervous about your first one, and honestly it means you care about doing it well. I can help with this because I have run our department meetings for the last three years and I have made basically every mistake there is.

If you want people to take the meeting seriously, you must send an agenda the day before. The reason I suggest this is that people arrive already thinking about the topics. If you skip it, half the room spends ten minutes asking what the meeting is about, and you lose control before you start.

Another step you ought to take is to decide the one decision you need by the end. Everything else is a discussion, not a decision. This keeps you from drifting, because when the conversation wanders you can simply point back at the thing you came to settle.

One final thing is, if I were in your shoes, I would arrive five minutes early and open the room myself. Standing there greeting people as they arrive turns you into the host rather than the person being watched.

You are going to be fine. Let me know how it goes.

Sample question 2: Getting back into exercise

Prompt

Your sister wants to start exercising again after two years away from it. Advise her on how to begin.

Model answer (about 90 seconds)

Hey Layla, I am really glad you brought this up. Two years is not that long, and starting again is much easier than starting from nothing. I can give you decent advice here because I have restarted three separate times and I have learned exactly which version of this fails.

If you want this to still be happening in March, you must start smaller than you think you should. The main reason is that the first two weeks are not about fitness at all, they are about proving to yourself that you will actually show up. Twenty minutes, three times a week, is a habit. Ninety minutes every day for eight days is a story you tell later about the time you quit.

Another thing you ought to do is book it into your calendar like a dentist appointment, with a specific day and a specific time. Something unscheduled competes with everything else in your week, and it loses.

Lastly, a suggestion I would make is that if I were you, I would find something you would do even in a bad mood. Walking, swimming, a class with a friend. Enjoyment is not a luxury here, it is the only thing that survives a busy month.

I know you will make the right choice. Good luck!

Sample question 3: Retaking a language test

Prompt

A friend missed the score they needed on an English test and is retaking it in three weeks. Give them advice on how to use the time.

Model answer (about 90 seconds)

Hi Priya, I was sorry to hear the score came back lower than you needed. Three weeks is genuinely enough time to move a band, so try not to panic. I can help because I have coached several people through exactly this window and I have seen what actually shifts a score in twenty-one days.

If you want a different result, you have to find out which section pulled you down before you study anything. This is critical. Most people react by studying everything a bit harder, which spreads three weeks across four skills and changes nothing. Your score report tells you where the gap is, so let it choose your timetable.

Then you ought to practise under the real time limits, with a timer running, from the first day rather than the last. Untimed practice teaches you the content but not the pressure, and the pressure is usually the thing that broke.

One final thing is, if I were in your position, I would stop new material four days before the test and just review. Cramming vocabulary the night before mostly costs you sleep, and tired candidates lose more marks than under-prepared ones.

I hope this helps. Call me if you want to run through anything.

Sample question 4: A first credit card

Prompt

A friend who recently arrived in Canada has been approved for their first credit card and wants to build a good credit history. Advise them.

Model answer (about 90 seconds)

Hi Omar, congratulations on the approval, that first card is harder to get than people expect. I am happy to guide you on this because I have been building my own credit here since I arrived and I have learned a few things the hard way.

First and foremost, if you want your score to go up rather than down, you absolutely must pay the full balance every month, not the minimum. The minimum payment keeps the account in good standing but leaves you paying interest on the rest, at a rate far higher than anything you would accept on a loan.

Another step you ought to take is to keep your spending well below your limit, somewhere around a third of it. This implies that if your limit is three thousand, you treat it as a thousand. Lenders look at how much of your available credit you use, not just whether you pay on time.

Finally, if I were in your position, I might consider setting up an automatic payment for the full statement amount. One missed due date can undo months of good behaviour, and automation removes the chance of forgetting.

You have got this. Let me know if you want to look at it together.

Sample question 5: Furnishing a first apartment

Prompt

Your neighbour is moving into their first unfurnished apartment and has a small budget. Give them advice on how to furnish it.

Model answer (about 90 seconds)

Hi Chen, congratulations on the new place. Furnishing from zero feels overwhelming, but it is very doable on a small budget. I can definitely give you some advice because I have furnished two apartments this way and I have wasted money on exactly the things you are about to be tempted by.

The first thing you must do is buy a decent bed and nothing else in week one. If you don't, you will spread the budget across ten cheap items and end up sleeping badly on all of them. You spend a third of your life on a mattress and about four minutes a day looking at a coffee table, so the money belongs in the bed.

It is generally a good idea to wait a month before buying storage. You genuinely do not know where the clutter collects in a new place until you have lived in it, and shelves bought on day two usually end up in the wrong room.

One final thing is, if I were in your shoes, I would check local buy-and-sell listings at the end of the month, when leases turn over. People moving out will almost give furniture away rather than pay to move it.

I can't wait to see how it turns out. Have fun!

Notice that none of these answers mention the test. They are just clear, specific advice from someone who has done the thing. That is what Task Fulfillment rewards, and it is the opposite of a recited script. If you want to hear how the same ideas sound in a less rehearsed register, we cover that in speaking naturally under pressure.

Universal Templates

Learn the shape of these lines, not the exact words. A sentence repeated word for word often sounds rehearsed, and it may not fit the person in your prompt.

For example, "The first thing you must do is [Action]" works for a friend furnishing an apartment. For your cousin who is moving to a city where she knows no one, keep the move and change the words: "The first thing you must do is find one group that meets every week."

Step 1: The Opening (Build Your Intro)

Combine A + B + C to create your opening.

A. The Greeting

  • "Hi [Name],"

B. The Context (Choose One)

Good News

  • "Congratulations on the [News]! It is so exciting to hear that..."

Bad News

  • "I was so sorry to hear about your [Problem]. That sounds really stressful..."

Decision

  • "Thanks for asking for my opinion. It is definitely a tough decision..."

C. The Validation / Experience (The "Why Me" Sentence)

Success

  • "I can definitely give you some advice because I have actually successfully [Activity] myself recently."

Failure

  • "I can certainly help you avoid mistakes because I have gone through this before and I learned a hard lesson."

Expertise

  • "I am happy to guide you because I have been working as a [Job Title] for over five years."

Step 2: The Main Advice (Mix & Match)

Select one template from each level to ensure variety.

Level 1: Strong Advice (Must / Have to)

  • "First and foremost, if you want to fix this, you absolutely must [Action]."
  • "To start with, you have to ensure that you [Action]. This is critical."
  • "The first thing you must do is [Action]. If you don't, you might fail."

Level 2: Practical Advice (Ought to / Should)

  • "Another step you ought to take is to [Action]."
  • "It is generally a good idea to [Action]."
  • "Moving on, you should also make sure that you [Action]."

Level 3: Hypothetical Advice (If I were you)

  • "One final thing is, if I were in your shoes, I would definitely [Action]."
  • "Finally, if I were in your position, I might consider [Action]."
  • "Lastly, a suggestion I would make is that if I were you, I would [Action]."

Support Connectors (Use these to explain WHY)

Reason

  • "The main reason I suggest this is that..."

Consequence

  • "This implies that if you do this, you will..."

Warning

  • "If you don't do this, you might risk..."

Step 3: The Closing

Keep it short and friendly.

  • "I know you will make the right choice. Good luck!"
  • "I hope this helps. Call me if you need anything else!"
  • "I can't wait to see how it turns out. Have fun!"

Speaking Task 1 Template FAQ

Quick answers to the questions test takers ask us most.

01

How long is CELPIP Speaking Task 1?

You get 30 seconds to prepare and 90 seconds to speak. CELPIP publishes both times in its 2026 Speaking Pro study pack. Task 1 and Task 7 are the two longest responses in the Speaking section; the other six give you 60 seconds.

02

Is Giving Advice Task 1 or Task 2 on the CELPIP test?

It is Task 1. CELPIP's Guidebook for Test Takers lists it first among the eight Speaking tasks, and the screen title reads "Speaking Task 1: Giving Advice". If you have seen it called "task 2", that label is not CELPIP's.

03

How many pieces of advice should you give?

At least three. Giving three or more suggestions, each backed by a reason or an example, is one of the five strategies CELPIP's study pack gives for Task 1. Three well-supported suggestions fill 90 seconds without padding. Two developed points still beat four rushed ones, but aim for three.

04

What does the examiner score on Task 1?

Four dimensions: Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Listenability and Task Fulfillment. CELPIP names these in its Speaking Pro study pack. There is no separate Grammar score on CELPIP Speaking (grammar is rated inside Listenability), and there is no "Coherence and Cohesion" band. Those are IELTS terms.

05

Where can you find CELPIP Speaking Task 1 sample questions with answers?

There are six full model answers on this page, and 84 more prompts in the Task 1 question bank, with the first one free.

06

Can you use a memorised template for Task 1?

Use it as a shape, not as a script. Two of the four scored dimensions are Content/Coherence and Vocabulary, and a recited answer fails both: the content stops matching the prompt, and the vocabulary stops sounding like yours. What you should memorise is the sequence, which is opening, three graded pieces of advice, close. Fill it fresh every time.

07

What are CELPIP's key strategies for Speaking Task 1?

CELPIP's study pack gives five. In our words: talk to the person as if they are in front of you, give three or more pieces of advice, back each one with a reason or an example, use a linking phrase each time you move to a new piece of advice, and sound kind and helpful. The table in "What does CELPIP itself say to do on Task 1?" shows where each one sits in the template.
