Top tips for scoring high on the CELPIP exam

Boost your CELPIP score with these top tips! Learn strategies to improve your performance in listening, reading, writing, and speaking, and maximize your test results.

To excel on the CELPIP test, mastering essential sentence structures for each section can help you communicate ideas clearly and concisely. Here are some valuable tips and sample sentences to guide you through the Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking sections of the CELPIP.

CELPIP Listening Tips

The Listening section presents multiple scenarios with increasing difficulty. Using strategic listening techniques and efficient note-taking skills is essential.

  • Organizing Notes with Symbols: Use symbols like “+” for positive and “-” for negative comments, or draw quick lines to separate key points from different speakers.

  • Practice Key Response Structures:

    • Identifying Agreement: “The speaker agrees that…”
    • Expressing Understanding: “It seems the speaker believes that…”
    • Reporting Observations: “In the conversation, the woman mentions…”

  • Listening to Details and Descriptions:

    • “The man in the blue shirt thinks…”
    • “The speaker on the left emphasizes…”

CELPIP Reading Tips

In the Reading section, being able to quickly summarize and interpret information can save time and keep your responses accurate.

  • Skimming and Scanning for Information: Start each paragraph with a purpose, then summarize main ideas using concise phrases.

  • Key Sentence Structures:

    • Interpreting Text: “According to the email, the main issue is…”
    • Identifying Point of View: “The writer expresses that…”
    • Summarizing Paragraphs: “This section explains how/why…”

  • Connecting Ideas:

    • “In this paragraph, the author compares…”
    • “The second paragraph elaborates on the point by discussing…”

CELPIP Writing Tips

In the Writing section, keeping your responses organized, formal, and clear is key. Using a structured approach will make your responses professional and easy to follow.

  • Writing an Email:

    • Introduction: “Dear [Name], I am reaching out to address…”
    • Purpose Statement: “I am writing to inform/request/clarify that…”
    • Main Points: “Firstly, I would like to mention…” or “In addition…”
    • Closing: “Thank you for your time and attention. I look forward to your response.”

  • Responding to a Survey Question:

    • Choosing a Side: “I strongly believe that Option A is the better choice because…”
    • Providing Reasons: “One of the primary reasons is that…”
    • Concluding Statement: “In light of these points, I am confident that Option A would benefit…”

  • Keep Responses within Word Count:

    • Write in short, focused sentences and avoid overly complex phrases.
    • Sample length: “Based on the reasons outlined, I am in full support of…”

CELPIP Speaking Tips

The Speaking section requires clear articulation and organized thoughts. Practicing with structured responses will help you manage time effectively for each task.

  • Task 1: Giving Advice

    • “If I were in your position, I would…”
    • “My advice is to consider…”
    • “It might also be helpful if you…”

  • Task 3: Describing a Scene

    • Overview: “This image shows a busy setting in a…”
    • Detailing Elements: “In the foreground, there is…; in the background, you can see…”
    • Spatial Descriptions: “To the left of the picture, there is…”

  • Task 5: Comparing and Persuading

    • Introducing Choice: “I understand that you prefer [Option A], but I think [Option B] might be more suitable because…”
    • Supporting with Reasons: “Firstly, [Option B] is more affordable/easier/more practical because…”
    • Conclusion: “For these reasons, I believe [Option B] would be a better choice.”

  • Task 7: Expressing Opinions

    • Stating Opinion: “I strongly believe that…”
    • Listing Reasons: “One reason is that…” or “Additionally, I think…”
    • Concluding: “In conclusion, this is why I feel…”

  • Task 8: Describing an Unusual Scene

    • Starting: “Hi [Name], I’m currently at [location], and there’s something very unusual here.”
    • Describing Uniqueness: “What’s surprising is…”
    • Adding Details: “I hope I gave you a clear idea of what I’m seeing.”

General Sentence Structures for CELPIP Preparation

  • Introducing Ideas:

    • “In my opinion,…”
    • “Based on what I have read/heard,…”
    • “To begin with,…”

  • Supporting Ideas:

    • “One of the main reasons is…”
    • “Another point to consider is…”

  • Concluding Statements:

    • “To sum up,…”
    • “In summary,…”
    • “For these reasons, I believe…”

Using these sentence structures and strategies can help you approach each section of the CELPIP test with confidence and clarity. Remember, organizing your responses, keeping your language simple and precise, and practicing with time limits can significantly improve your performance.

Good to know

Frequently Asked Questions

1

What is the best way to prepare for CELPIP?

Learn the format first, then practise under real conditions, then fix your mistakes. Read the test format guide so nothing on test day surprises you, take one timed practice test in each section to find your baseline, and spend most of your study time on the two sections where you lost the most points. Four weeks is enough for most test takers at an intermediate level; the 4-week CELPIP study plan lays it out day by day.

2

How to improve CELPIP scores?

Stop taking more practice tests and start analysing the ones you have taken. For every wrong answer, write down the correct answer, why you picked the wrong one, and what you will do differently. In Listening and Reading, the pattern is usually a question type (viewpoints, paraphrased details). In Writing and Speaking, it is usually one scoring criterion, such as not answering every part of the task or repeating the same vocabulary. Fix the pattern, then test again.

3

Is it hard to get 7 in CELPIP?

A 7 means CLB 7, and most Express Entry programs want it in all four skills. In Reading it is about 24 to 28 correct out of 38. In Writing and Speaking it means a response that answers every part of the task, stays organised, and uses clear, varied language without frequent errors. (pedagogy) For someone who uses English daily, a 7 is reachable with a few weeks of focused practice. The usual reason people miss it is not their English, it is the format: running out of time, skipping a bullet point in the email task, or not filling the speaking time.

4

What is the hardest part in CELPIP?

In our teaching, the parts test takers struggle with most are Listening Parts 5 and 6, where you track several speakers or several viewpoints from a single monologue, and the Speaking tasks, where 30 to 60 seconds of preparation and 60 to 90 seconds of speaking leave no room to pause and think. Reading Part 4 (viewpoints) is the usual weak spot in Reading. Which one is hardest for you depends on your background, which is why a timed baseline in each section is the first step of any plan.

5

Is CELPIP 10 a good score?

Yes. CELPIP 10 equals CLB 10, and CLB 10 or higher earns the maximum CRS points for language in each ability. In Listening and Reading, levels 10, 11 and 12 are reported as a single top band, so a 10 there is the highest result on the scale. For any immigration program, a 10 is well above every minimum.

6

What is 8777 IELTS equal in CELPIP?

IELTS 8777 (Listening 8.0, Reading 7.0, Writing 7.0, Speaking 7.0) is CLB 9 in all four skills, and CLB 9 is CELPIP 9 in each skill. CELPIP levels map one to one to CLB, so the CELPIP equivalent of 8777 is a 9 in Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. See the full CELPIP to CLB table for other combinations.
