What is the CELPIP Reading test format?

The CELPIP Reading test has four parts, 38 scored questions, and runs 43 to 56 minutes. Part 1 is everyday correspondence, such as emails and letters. Part 2 asks you to read a diagram, chart, or schedule and apply it to a short message. Part 3 is a longer informational passage that tests both detail and inference. Part 4 gives you several viewpoints on one topic and checks whether you can tell the opinions apart.

Every question is multiple choice, and every passage uses Canadian English drawn from work, community, and daily-life situations. You manage your own time across the four parts. There is no forced per-part clock, so pacing is part of the skill.

How many questions are on the CELPIP Reading test?

The CELPIP Reading test has 38 scored questions. They are spread across the four parts, and each correct answer is worth one point toward your raw score out of 38. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so answer every question even when you are unsure. A blank and a wrong guess cost you the same, and a guess can still be right.

Try a sample CELPIP Reading question (with answer and explanation)

Here is a short Part 1 (Correspondence) sample so you know what to expect before you start.

Passage. Hi Dana, thanks again for covering my Saturday shift. I've swapped you onto the early Thursday slot as we agreed, but payroll needs the change in writing by Wednesday or it won't process this cycle. Could you reply to confirm? Thanks, Priya

Question. What does Priya need Dana to do by Wednesday?

  • a) Work the Saturday shift
  • b) Reply in writing to confirm the swap
  • c) Contact payroll directly
  • d) Choose a different Thursday slot

Answer: b) Reply in writing to confirm the swap. The message says payroll needs the change "in writing by Wednesday," and Priya asks Dana directly to "reply to confirm." Option c is the trap: payroll is mentioned, but Priya is the one handling them, not Dana. Ready for a full set? Start Part 1 now.

How is the CELPIP Reading test scored?

Your 38-question raw score converts to a CELPIP Reading level from 0 to 12, and from level 3 up, that level equals your Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) level one-to-one, so a CELPIP 9 is CLB 9. As a rough guide, about 24 of 38 correct maps to Level 7, 28 to Level 8, and 31 to Level 9.

Correct (of 38)CELPIP / CLB levelWhat it signals
31 to 339Strong; competitive for most Express Entry draws
28 to 318Good detailed comprehension
24 to 287Meets the common Express Entry minimum

Bands are approximate, calibrated to public Paragon score-comparison tables; the official test is statistically equated per version, so treat these as a close guide. For the full M-to-12 chart, the IELTS/TOEFL cross-reference, and how each level reads, see the CELPIP Reading score chart.

What CELPIP Reading score do I need for Express Entry or PR?

Most Express Entry programs need CLB 7 in Reading, which covers the Federal Skilled Worker program and Canadian Experience Class for TEER 0 and 1 jobs. The Federal Skilled Trades program and Canadian Experience Class TEER 2 and 3 accept CLB 5 in Reading. IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) scores each skill on its own, so your Reading level has to clear the bar by itself. A strong Speaking score will not carry it.

Higher Reading levels also earn more points under the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS), so aim past the floor: if CLB 7 is the minimum, target CLB 9. Confirm the current minimums for your program on the minimum CELPIP scores for each immigration program guide and at ircc.canada.ca. Note that Canadian citizenship uses CELPIP-General LS, which tests Listening and Speaking only, so it has no Reading section.

How can I improve my CELPIP Reading score?

Read the questions before the passage so you know what you are hunting for, then skim for those answers instead of reading every word. Watch your pacing: a workable split is roughly 11 minutes for Part 1, 9 for Part 2, 10 for Part 3, and 13 for Part 4. Inference questions cause the most errors, so slow down when the answer is not stated word-for-word. And whenever you miss one, read the full explanation before moving on. That is where the score gains hide.

Build the habit with structure. The free CELPIP Reading course walks through each part's tactics, and the CELPIP Reading tips and strategies guide covers skim-and-scan and time management in more depth. Then test yourself under a timer until the pacing feels automatic.

Is CELPIP Reading easier than IELTS?

CELPIP and IELTS Reading differ in content, not really in difficulty. CELPIP uses practical Canadian material like workplace emails, schedules, news items, and community notices, while IELTS Academic leans on scholarly passages. CELPIP is fully computer-based with a fixed four-part format, so many test-takers find it more predictable to prepare for. If you are deciding between the two tests, the CELPIP vs IELTS comparison breaks down the trade-offs.
