CELPIP Speaking Task 8: The 3-Line Note That Fills 60 Seconds

What is CELPIP Speaking Task 8?

In CELPIP Speaking Task 8, Describing an Unusual Situation, you get 30 seconds to prepare and 60 seconds to speak. You describe something unusual to a person who cannot see it. Give a reason for calling, describe it from the whole thing to the details, then answer the question in the prompt. Raters score Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Listenability and Task Fulfillment.

Goal

Describe a picture of an unusual item or situation to someone who cannot see it, such as a friend, a family member or a store clerk, as if you were on the phone. They should be able to picture it when you finish. Clear, exact words do that better than rare ones.

What do CELPIP raters score on Task 8?

CELPIP rates every Speaking response on four dimensions: Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Listenability and Task Fulfillment. There is no separate grammar score. Grammar and sentence structure are one part of Listenability, together with pronunciation, rhythm and pauses.

What does each dimension mean on Task 8?

Here is what each one covers, in our own short summary, and what it looks like in a Task 8 answer:

DimensionIn short (our summary)What it means on Task 8
Content/CoherenceYour ideas, how well they are organized, and the details behind themThe whole thing first, then the parts in a fixed order, each placed in relation to the others
VocabularyPicking exact words that suit the topic, with some rangePosition words (between, on top of, behind) and exact colours, sizes and materials
ListenabilityHow easy you are to follow: delivery, pace, grammar and sentence varietyShort sentences for each part, a few longer ones joined with "so" or "which", and a steady pace

Three habits help on Task 8:

Sound like you mean it. A line like "I haven't seen anything like this before!" (Present Perfect) sounds warmer than "I didn't see this before." It is a teaching tip, not a rule the raters check.

Pick precise words. "Sizable", "vibrant" or "shaped like a cupcake" paint a clearer picture than "big", "nice" or "cool". Precise beats fancy: plain words used exactly, like "a yellow sign" or "a red post box", give your listener a clearer picture than rare words used loosely.

Set the scene in one or two sentences, then describe. Tell your listener why you are calling, then move to the description. Do not spend half your minute on the story: the description is what the task asks for.

Step 1: The Setup (Tone, Story & Grammar)

Goal: Determine your feeling (Love vs. Interest), build the story, and use Present Perfect to sound natural.

A. The "Excitement Check"

The Prompt

"Whimsical candy playground."

Your Reaction

Fun/Exciting. → Enthusiastic Tone.

B. The Story Formula (Using Present Perfect)

Instead of starting with "I am here," connect it to the past using Have been.

Weak (Simple Past/Present)

Strong (Present Perfect)

  • Weak (Simple Past/Present):

    "Hi Sarah. You wanted a park. I found one."

    Strong (Present Perfect):

    "Hi Sarah. I have been thinking about what you said regarding a new park for the kids, and I think I have finally found the perfect spot."

Step 2: The Visual Tour (General → Specific)

Goal:

Describe the object logically so the listener can visualize it. Do not jump around. Start with one sentence about the whole thing, then go part by part and say where each part is: at the front, in the middle, on top of, next to. Your listener builds the picture in the order you give it. CELPIP's highest rated sample for this task works exactly like this, in our reading of it: an overview first, then each object placed in relation to the others.

Step 3: The Closing (The Specific Question)

Goal: You must end the call naturally by asking the specific question found in the prompt.

Don't just say goodbye.

Read the task instructions again in your prep time: they tell you what to do at the end, for example ask a question, make a suggestion or ask for help. Do that, then end the call with one short goodbye line.

Example

"Do you think the kids would enjoy playing here, or is it a bit too colorful for them?"

CELPIP Speaking Task 8: Model Answer

What does a CELPIP Speaking Task 8 question look like?

The real screen shows a picture of something unusual and a short task above it: who you are calling, why, and what to do at the end. Here is one in that format, written by us.

Task: You are at a weekend market and you see an unusual bicycle for sale. Your neighbour Dan wants a way to carry groceries without a car. Call Dan, describe the bicycle, and ask if he wants the seller's phone number.

The picture shows: a black city bicycle with a large wooden box fixed between the handlebars and the front wheel. Four flower pots sit in the box. There is a strap on each side of the box, and a small child seat with a cushion behind the rider's seat.

Here is a 60 second answer that uses the template on this page (about 150 words):

Hi Dan, it's me. Last week you said you wanted a way to carry groceries without the car, and I think I have just found it. I'm at the Saturday market, and I have never seen a bike like this before.

So, picture a normal black city bike. But at the front, between the handlebars and the front wheel, there is a big wooden box, about the size of a laundry basket. It sits low, close to the ground, so it would not tip over easily. Right now the seller has four flower pots in it, so it clearly holds a lot. There is a strap on each side of the box to stop things from sliding out. And behind the seat there is a little child seat with a soft cushion.

It looks a bit funny, but it looks really practical. Do you want me to get the seller's number? Okay, I'll text it to you. Bye!

What makes this answer work: one sentence for the whole bike, then each part placed in relation to the others (at the front, between, on each side, behind). "About the size of a laundry basket" uses common words for a size that is hard to name, which is one of CELPIP's own strategies. The call has a reason, a clear end, and a real goodbye. A weaker answer lists the parts in random order, uses "nice" and "big" for everything, and is still describing when the minute runs out.

Universal Templates

Use these openers and adjectives as a starting point, then change them to fit each picture. Raters notice when an answer is recited: a memorised line in the wrong tone, or about the wrong thing, costs you on Task Fulfillment.

1. The Story Opener(Present Perfect)

  • "Hi [Name]. I know you have been looking for [Object]..."
  • "Hi [Name]. I have been thinking about our conversation regarding [Topic]..."

2. The Reaction(Emotional Connection)

  • "I haven't seen anything this unique in a long time!"
  • "I have never come across such a stunning design before."

  • 3. Size Adjectives (Replace "Big")

    • Sizable
    • Massive
    • Colossal
    • Mammoth

  • 4. Beauty Adjectives (Replace "Pretty")

    • Stunning
    • Enchanting
    • Exquisite
    • Vibrant

  • 5. Interest Adjectives (Replace "Interesting")

    • Fascinating
    • Intriguing
    • Captivating
    • Unique

  • 6. Quality Adjectives (Replace "Good")

    • Superb
    • Magnificent
    • Splendid
    • Marvelous

  • **When you do not know the word (build it from simple words)

    • It's a kind of [box / chair / lamp] with a [handle / wheel / light] on top.
    • It looks like a [cupcake / boat / giant shoe].
    • It's made of [wood / metal / plastic], and it's about the size of a [fridge / basketball / car].
    • It's the part you hold when you [open it / carry it].

Speaking Task 8 Template FAQ

Quick answers to the questions test takers ask us most.

01

How long is CELPIP Speaking Task 8?

30 seconds to prepare and 60 seconds to speak, according to CELPIP's Speaking Pro study pack.

02

What do you do in CELPIP Speaking Task 8?

You describe something unusual to a person who cannot see it, such as a friend or a store clerk, and finish by doing what the prompt asks, for example making a suggestion or asking a question.

03

Can I use a template for Task 8?

Yes, as a plan for the order of your answer: reason for calling, the whole thing, the details, the closing question. Do not memorise full sentences. A recited answer often misses what the prompt asks, and that costs you on Task Fulfillment.

For the bicycle question above, "I haven't seen anything this unique in a long time!" became "I have never seen a bike like this before." Same move, words that fit the picture.

04

Can I practise a Task 8 question for free?

Yes. Sign up free and answer the first Speaking Task 8 question on the real clock, with AI feedback, up to three tries.

05

What are CELPIP's key strategies for Speaking Task 8?

CELPIP gives five. In our words: open the phone call and say why you are calling, spend your time on what makes the picture unusual, talk about the parts you can describe well, use simple words to build a phrase when you do not know the exact one, and finish the call properly.

06

What if I do not know the word for something in the Task 8 picture?

Describe it with common words: what it looks like, what it is made of, how big it is and where it is. "A metal box with a handle on top, about the size of a microwave" is clear to the listener. Stopping to search for the exact word costs you more than the word is worth.

07

Do I have to end the phone call in CELPIP Speaking Task 8?

Yes, plan for it. Finishing the call is one of CELPIP's own strategies for this task, and an answer that is cut off in the middle of the description has not done everything the task asks. Do what the prompt asks at the end, then say goodbye, all inside the 60 seconds.
