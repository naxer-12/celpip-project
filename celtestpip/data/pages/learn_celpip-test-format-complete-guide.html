CELPIP Test Format 2026: Parts, Questions and Time Limits

Complete breakdown of the CELPIP test format for 2026. Every section, part, question type, and time limit for Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking.

The CELPIP-General test has four sections and takes about 3 hours in one sitting. Listening is 6 parts and 38 scored questions in 46 to 55 minutes. Reading is 4 parts and 38 scored questions in 43 to 56 minutes. Writing is 2 tasks in 53 minutes. Speaking is 8 tasks in 15 minutes.

The CELPIP test can feel overwhelming when you don't know what's coming. Understanding the format is half the battle. Once you know exactly what each section looks like, how many questions you'll face, and how much time you have, you can walk into the test centre with confidence.

Key facts about the CELPIP format:

  • The entire test is computer-based, and there is no examiner in the room. Your Writing and Speaking answers are recorded and rated later by trained raters. You do get paper and a pen at the test centre for notes, but you can't write or highlight on the screen.
  • You complete all four sections in one sitting: Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking.
  • Once you move to the next section, you cannot go back.
  • Each section is scored independently on a scale of 0 to 12, which from level 3 up maps directly to CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) levels.
  • There is no penalty for wrong answers, so always answer every question.

What Is the Order of the CELPIP Test?

You sit all four sections back to back in a single sitting, and the whole thing takes about 3 hours.

Every section carries its own published time range, so the clocks are separate. Our read of that is that you cannot bank time: finishing Reading quickly does not hand you longer for Writing, so plan each section against its own range rather than against the 3 hours.

The order you will see published almost everywhere, and the order we run our practice sets in, is Listening, then Reading, then Writing, then Speaking. We would prepare in that sequence.

Practice a full section under the real clock. Start with Reading, where the pacing catches most people out.

How Long Does the CELPIP Test Take?

About 3 hours in total, in one sitting. Section by section, the official figures are 46 to 55 minutes for Listening, 43 to 56 minutes for Reading, 53 minutes for Writing and 15 minutes for Speaking.

Two of those are ranges rather than fixed numbers, which unsettles people trying to plan a day off work. No official source we can find explains why, Paragon included.

What Paragon does say is this: the Listening and Reading tests may contain unscored content used for test development, and it has the same format as the scored content. Our read of that is simple. You will not be able to pick the unscored material out while you are sitting there, so there is no point trying.

Plan for the top of every range. If you budget 55 minutes for Listening and 56 for Reading you will never be caught short, and you will not spend the exam doing arithmetic instead of listening.

CELPIP Listening Section Format

The Listening section has 6 scored parts plus 1 unscored Practice Task. Your test may also contain other unscored content used for test development, and Paragon says you will not be able to tell it apart from the scored content. You'll hear each audio clip only once, and there's no replay button.

Practice Task (Not Scored)

Before the real test begins, you get a quick warm-up with a short audio clip. This helps you adjust to the volume, headset, and pace. It's not scored, but use it to make sure your audio is working properly.

Part 1: Listening to Problem Solving

DetailInfo
Questions8
Timeabout 8 minutes
Audio3 conversations (1 to 1.5 min each)
Questions per clip2 to 3

You'll hear everyday conversations where people are solving problems: discussing travel plans, scheduling, or making decisions. Questions test your ability to understand practical details such as numbers, times, prices, and solutions.

Tip: Listen for the final decision or agreed-upon plan. The speakers often discuss multiple options before settling on one.

Part 2: Listening to a Daily Life Conversation

DetailInfo
Questions5
Timeabout 5 minutes
Audio1 conversation (1.5 to 2 min)

A casual conversation between two people about everyday topics. This tests your understanding of opinions, attitudes, and implied meaning, not just the literal words.

Tip: Pay attention to tone of voice. Speakers might say one thing but mean another. Listen for phrases like "I suppose," "to be honest," or "actually". These signal the speaker's real opinion.

Part 3: Listening for Information

DetailInfo
Questions6
Timeabout 6 minutes
Audio1 clip (2 to 2.5 min)

A conversation between two people, one man and one woman, where one of them has information the other wants: a salesperson walking a customer through a new car, an experienced employee explaining a procedure to a new one. Questions focus on explanations, sequences, and specific details.

Tip: The information comes in a logical order. If you miss something, keep listening, because you can often figure out the answer from context.

Part 4: Listening to a News Item

DetailInfo
Questions5
Timeabout 5 minutes
Audio1 news report (about 1.5 min)

A short news-style report similar to what you'd hear on the radio. Tests your ability to understand facts, causes, statistics, and reported information.

Tip: News items follow a predictable structure: what happened, who's involved, why it matters. Listen for numbers and statistics, which often appear in questions.

Part 5: Listening to a Discussion

DetailInfo
Questions8
Timeabout 9 minutes
Audio1 video discussion (1.5 to 2 min) with 3 speakers

A video clip of three people discussing a topic. The challenge is tracking who said what while following the conversation.

Tip: Before the audio plays, note the speakers' names. During the discussion, focus on each speaker's main position. Questions often ask "Who believes...?" or "Which speaker agrees with...?"

Part 6: Listening for Viewpoints

DetailInfo
Questions6
Timeabout 8 minutes
Audio1 monologue (about 3 min)

A single speaker talks at length about a topic. This is the longest audio clip and tests your ability to distinguish opinions from facts, understand reasoning, and follow an extended argument.

Tip: The speaker will often present both sides of an issue before giving their view. Listen for signal phrases like "I believe," "the evidence shows," or "some might argue" to distinguish facts from opinions.

How Many Parts Are There in CELPIP Reading?

CELPIP Reading has 4 parts and 38 scored questions, and it runs 43 to 56 minutes. Inside each part you can change your answers as many times as you like until that part's time runs out. Then the test moves on by itself, and you can't go back to an earlier part.

Part 1: Reading Correspondence

DetailInfo
Questions11 (6 + 5)
Suggested pacingabout 11 minutes
FormatEmail exchange or written correspondence

You'll read a set of emails or messages and answer questions in two groups. The first group tests comprehension. The second may ask you to complete a response.

Tip: Read the emails carefully for who is writing to whom and why. The questions often test whether you understand the purpose, tone, and specific requests in the messages.

Part 2: Reading to Apply a Diagram

DetailInfo
Questions8 (5 + 3)
Suggested pacingabout 9 minutes
FormatText plus diagram, chart, or visual

You'll read a passage alongside a diagram, map, or visual aid. Questions test your ability to connect written information with the visual.

Tip: Scan the diagram first to understand its layout. Then read the text. Many questions require you to match specific details from the text to locations or elements in the diagram.

Part 3: Reading for Information

DetailInfo
Questions9
Suggested pacingabout 10 minutes
FormatLong passage (multiple paragraphs)

A longer text where you need to find specific pieces of information. Each question asks which paragraph contains a particular detail.

Tip: Skim first, then scan. Read each paragraph heading or first sentence to build a mental map of where different topics are discussed. Then go to the questions and scan for the specific detail.

Part 4: Reading for Viewpoints

DetailInfo
Questions10
Suggested pacingabout 13 minutes
FormatOpinion-based text with multiple viewpoints

You'll read a text where different viewpoints are expressed: letters to the editor, forum posts, or reviews. Questions test whether you can identify who holds which opinion.

Tip: This is similar to Listening Part 5 but in written form. Underline or mentally note each person's main argument. Questions often ask about agreement, disagreement, or specific positions.

For part-by-part Reading strategies, check out our CELPIP Reading tips guide. Practice with Reading questions on CELTESTPIP.

Those per-part times are our suggested pacing, not official limits. CELPIP gives you one clock for the whole Reading section and lets you move between parts however you like.

How Many Tasks Are in CELPIP Writing?

The Writing section has just 2 tasks. Both are typed on the computer, and both require 150 to 200 words. There is a built-in spell checker.

Task 1: Writing an Email

DetailInfo
Time27 minutes
Word count150 to 200 words
FormatEmail response to a prompt

You'll receive a scenario and must write an email addressing specific points outlined in the prompt. The prompt typically gives you 3 bullet points to cover.

What you need to do:

Address all bullet points in the prompt, and don't skip any.

Use the right tone for the person you are writing to: formal for a manager, a company or someone you don't know, informal for a friend or family member.

Organise your email with a clear opening, body, and closing.

Stay within the word count.

Tip: Spend 3 to 4 minutes planning before you write. Identify the tone first. Is it to a friend, a manager, or a business? Then allocate one paragraph per bullet point. Save 3 minutes at the end to review for grammar and spelling mistakes.

Task 2: Responding to Survey Questions

DetailInfo
Time26 minutes
Word count150 to 200 words
FormatOpinion-based response

You'll be given a question with two options and must choose one, then explain and justify your choice with reasons and examples.

What you need to do:

Choose one option clearly in your opening.

Provide 2 to 3 strong reasons for your choice.

Support each reason with specific examples or explanations.

Briefly acknowledge the other option. Optional, but it shows sophistication.

Tip: Don't overthink which option to choose. Pick the one you can write about most easily. The evaluators care about how well you express your opinion, not which option you picked.

How Many Tasks Are in CELPIP Speaking?

The Speaking section has 8 tasks and runs 15 minutes. You speak into a microphone, with no examiner in the room. Your recordings are rated later by trained raters. For each task, you get preparation time to think, then response time to speak.

This is where many test takers feel nervous, but remember: nobody is watching you while you speak. Take a breath and speak naturally.

Task 1: Giving Advice

DetailInfo
Prep time30 seconds
Speaking time90 seconds

Someone needs your advice on a decision or situation. Give practical, specific advice.

Task 2: Talking About a Personal Experience

DetailInfo
Prep time30 seconds
Speaking time60 seconds

Describe a personal experience related to the prompt. Use specific details to make it vivid.

Task 3: Describing a Scene

DetailInfo
Prep time30 seconds
Speaking time60 seconds

You'll see an image and must describe what's happening. Cover who, what, where, and what might happen next.

Task 4: Making Predictions

DetailInfo
Prep time30 seconds
Speaking time60 seconds

Based on a scenario, predict what will happen and explain why.

Task 5: Comparing and Persuading

DetailInfo
Prep time60s + 60s (60s to choose, then 60s to prepare)
Speaking time60 seconds

Compare two options and persuade someone to choose one. State your preference clearly and give reasons.

Task 6: Dealing with a Difficult Situation

DetailInfo
Prep time60 seconds
Speaking time60 seconds

You're in a challenging situation and need to address it. Show diplomacy and problem-solving skills.

Task 7: Expressing Opinions

DetailInfo
Prep time30 seconds
Speaking time90 seconds

Share your opinion on a topic and support it with reasons and examples. This is one of two tasks with a longer speaking time.

Task 8: Describing an Unusual Situation

DetailInfo
Prep time30 seconds
Speaking time60 seconds

Describe what's happening in an unusual scene and suggest explanations for it.

How CELPIP Scoring Works: Score Scale and CLB Levels

Each of the four sections is scored on a scale of 0 to 12. These scores map directly to CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) levels, so a CELPIP 9 = CLB 9.

CELPIP levelCLB level
10 to 12CLB 10 to 12
7 to 9CLB 7 to 9
4 to 6CLB 4 to 6
3CLB 3
2CLB 1 to 2
0 and 1Not enough information to give a level

Scoring is independent per section. You could score 10 in Listening and 7 in Writing. Each skill is evaluated separately. For immigration purposes, you must meet the minimum CLB in every skill.

Results are usually available within a few business days after the test. Paragon's own pages currently quote different turnarounds, so check the figure shown when you book. For immigration, IRCC accepts results that are less than two years old. For a full breakdown of how scores affect your immigration application, see our CELPIP scores and CLB guide.

CELPIP Test Day Quick Reference

Here's everything you need to remember on test day:

Before the test:

Arrive 45 minutes early. You'll be denied entry if you're more than 15 minutes late, with no refund.

Bring your original, valid government-issued ID, the same one used to register.

Leave all personal items at home or in a locker. Nothing is allowed in the test room except your ID.

During the test:

  • The test goes in order: Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking.
  • No breaks between sections, so use the washroom before you start.
  • There's a timer on screen for every section.
  • In Listening and Reading you can change an answer until that part's time runs out, but you can't go back to an earlier part.
  • A spell checker is available in the Writing section.

After the test:

  • Results usually will be ready within a few business days. Check your CELPIP account online.
  • Score reports can be sent directly to IRCC for immigration applications.

Frequently Asked Questions

1

How long is the CELPIP test in total?

The CELPIP-General test takes approximately 3 hours from start to finish, including all four sections. With check-in time, plan for about 3.5 to 4 hours at the test centre.

2

Is there a CELPIP test format PDF?

Yes. Our free CELPIP test format PDF puts the whole 2026 format on one page: the 6 Listening parts, 4 Reading parts, 2 Writing tasks and 8 Speaking tasks, with the number of questions, the time for each part and the preparation and speaking time for each Speaking task. You can download and print it without signing up, and every part on it links to a free practice set.

3

Can I take CELPIP sections on different days?

No, all four sections must be completed in one sitting on the same day. You cannot split them across multiple sessions.

4

Is there a break during the CELPIP test?

There is no official break. The test runs continuously through all four sections. You can use the washroom during the test by raising your hand, but the timer will not pause. Use the washroom before the test starts.

5

Can I go back and change answers on the CELPIP test?

Only inside the part you are on. In Listening and Reading, you can change your answers as many times as you like until that part's time runs out. Then the test moves on by itself, and you cannot go back to an earlier part or an earlier section.

6

Is the CELPIP Speaking section face-to-face?

No. You speak into a microphone connected to the computer, with no examiner in the room. Your recordings are rated later by trained raters. Practise with Speaking questions on CELTESTPIP to get comfortable with this format.

7

What happens if I don't finish a CELPIP section in time?

The test automatically moves to the next section when time runs out. Any unanswered questions count as blank, so always answer every question, even if you have to guess. There's no penalty for wrong answers.

8

Can I take notes during the CELPIP test?

Yes, you receive an erasable noteboard and marker at the test centre for taking notes throughout the entire test. These must be returned after the test. Using the noteboard is one of the most effective strategies for the Listening section.

9

Is the CELPIP Online test the same as the in-person test?

The format and content are the same, but CELPIP Online cannot be used for immigration or citizenship purposes. If you need scores for IRCC applications, you must take the test at an official test centre. See our CELPIP scores and immigration guide for details on program requirements.

10

How should I prepare for the CELPIP test format?

Start by practicing each section under timed conditions. Use CELTESTPIP's practice tests for all four skills, and follow our 4-week CELPIP study plan for a structured preparation schedule. Build your vocabulary with the CELPIP word lists to improve Reading and Listening comprehension.
