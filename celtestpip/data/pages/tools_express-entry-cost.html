By Matthew Zare, CELPIP teacher at CELTESTPIPChecked November 19, 2026 against IRCC's fee list

IRCC's fees for one Express Entry applicant come to $1,675: $1,590 for the application, including the right of permanent residence fee, and $85 for biometrics. A couple pays $3,350, and each dependent child adds $270.

Those are the government's fees only. Language tests, an education assessment, medical exams and police certificates come on top. The calculator above adds IRCC's fees for your family and lets you fill in the rest.

How much does Express Entry cost in 2026?

$1,590 for each adult, $270 for each dependent child, plus biometrics. These are IRCC's fees for every Express Entry program: Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class and Federal Skilled Trades.

What does Express Entry cost for a family?

FamilyIRCC fees
Single applicant$1,675
Couple$3,350
Couple, two children (one 14 or older)$3,890

The couple's total is 2 × $1,590 plus $170 for biometrics. With two children, add 2 × $270. The biometrics fee stays at $170 however many of you give them.

When do you pay the fees?

When you submit your application for permanent residence, after an invitation to apply. You pay online in your IRCC account by debit or credit card (IRCC, apply for permanent residence).

  • At the latest when you submit: the processing fees and the biometrics fee.
  • Can wait: the right of permanent residence fee. IRCC asks for it before you become a permanent resident, but paying it with the rest avoids delays.

IRCC's steps to create an Express Entry profile include no payment: the fees come only if you are invited and apply.

Which fees can you get back?

  • An incomplete application is returned with the processing fee and the right of PR fee, if you paid it.
  • Once processing starts, IRCC does not refund the processing fee.
  • The right of PR fee is refunded if you withdraw your application or IRCC refuses it. It is the only fee IRCC can refund after processing has started.

Source: IRCC, fee list and apply for permanent residence.

Who pays the biometrics fee?

Most permanent residence applicants give biometrics, and most must give them every time they apply. Children 13 or under and people 80 or older when they apply are exempt (IRCC, biometrics). A family applying at the same time pays $170 at most, however many of you give them.

What other costs should you plan for?

Outside IRCC, these are the usual costs. They depend on your country and the provider, so the calculator lets you type them in.

  • Language test. Every Express Entry program needs one. A CELPIP-General test is $299 plus tax in Canada; see CELPIP cost and fees by province. A retake costs the same again, so preparation pays for itself.
  • Educational Credential Assessment (ECA). Needed for education from outside Canada, from an IRCC-designated organization.
  • Medical exams. You and every family member, even those not coming, see an IRCC panel physician. You pay the physician directly.
  • Police certificates. For you and each family member 18 or older, from every country where you stayed 6 months in a row or longer in the last 10 years (IRCC, police certificates).
  • Translations. Only for documents that are not in English or French, by a certified translator.

Is proof of funds part of the cost?

No. You keep that money. Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades candidates must show settlement funds, $15,263 for one person and $28,362 for a family of four, unless they can work in Canada and have a job offer. The Canadian Experience Class needs none. The proof of funds calculator has the full table, and the eligibility checker tells you which programs you qualify for.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Express Entry cost for one person?

$1,675 in IRCC fees. That is the $990 processing fee, the $600 right of permanent residence fee and $85 for biometrics. Language tests, an ECA, a medical exam and police certificates are extra.

How much does Express Entry cost for a couple?

$3,350 in IRCC fees. $1,590 for each adult (processing plus the right of permanent residence fee) and $170 for the family's biometrics. Each dependent child adds $270.

Do I have to pay the right of permanent residence fee upfront?

No, but IRCC suggests it. You can pay the right of permanent residence fee when IRCC approves your application, before you become a permanent resident. Paying it with your application avoids delays, and it is refunded if you withdraw or are refused.

Is there a fee to create an Express Entry profile?

IRCC lists no fee for it. IRCC's steps to create a profile and enter the pool include no payment. Its fees are paid when you are invited and submit your application for permanent residence.

Do dependent children pay the right of permanent residence fee?

No. Dependent children pay only the $270 processing fee. The right of permanent residence fee does not apply to the dependent children of a principal applicant.
