CELPIP Listening Format: 6 Parts and What Each Sounds Like

CELPIP Listening has 6 scored parts, 38 questions and 46 to 55 minutes. What each part sounds like, how the questions work, and the 3 question types.

The CELPIP Listening test has 6 scored parts, 38 questions, and takes 46 to 55 minutes. Every clip plays once. The questions come after the audio, with four options each. In Parts 1 to 3 you hear the questions one at a time. In Parts 4 to 6 you read them all on one screen and answer in any order.

What is the CELPIP Listening test format?

Listening is the first section of the CELPIP-General test and of the CELPIP-General LS test, and the format is the same in both. You sit at a computer with a headset, the audio starts on its own, and you cannot pause it or play it again. After each clip, the questions appear.

CELPIP's own overview, published in the Listening Pro Study Pack, lists the parts like this. The minutes are approximate figures from that document, and the current Guidebook gives the whole section as 46 to 55 minutes.

PartWhat you hearQuestionsTime (about)
Practice Task (not scored)one short clip to get used to the audio11 minute
Part 1: Listening to Problem Solvinga conversation in three pieces88 minutes
Part 2: Listening to a Daily Life Conversationone conversation55 minutes
Part 3: Listening for Informationone conversation66 minutes
Part 4: Listening to a News Itemone speaker, a news report55 minutes
Part 5: Listening to a Discussionone video, three speakers89 minutes
Part 6: Listening for Viewpointsone speaker, a prepared talk68 minutes

Add those up and you get 38 scored questions. The part minutes are CELPIP's approximate figures and they come to less than the 46 to 55 minutes the section is timed at, partly because any unscored content you meet on the day counts toward the total.

Two facts about the whole section shape everything else. First, the clips get harder as you go: CELPIP states that the audio passages and questions increase in difficulty from Part 1 to Part 6. Second, there is no going back. Once a part's time is up, the test moves you forward on its own, and a previous part is closed for good.

What does each CELPIP Listening part sound like?

Knowing the count is the easy half. What actually helps on the day is knowing who is talking, for how long, and about what, before the audio starts. This is what each part sounds like, in the shape CELPIP describes it.

Part 1: Listening to Problem Solving

Two speakers, one man and one woman, and they do not know each other. Think of a customer asking a cashier about a return, or two coworkers where one has a problem at home and the other wants to help. The conversation is split into three sections of about 1 to 1.5 minutes each, and after each section you get 2 or 3 questions about what you just heard. That makes Part 1 the gentlest opening: the clips are short and the questions arrive while the details are fresh. Practise it on the Part 1 question bank.

Part 2: Listening to a Daily Life Conversation

Two speakers again, but this time they know each other: coworkers on a project, teammates before a game, a couple planning the weekend. It is one clip of about 1.5 to 2 minutes, then about 5 questions. The tone is friendly and polite but not personal. The trap here is that you hear the whole conversation before any question, so your notes have to carry you. The Part 2 questions are the place to build that habit.

Part 3: Listening for Information

Still a conversation between two people, one man and one woman. One of them knows something the other wants: a salesperson walking a customer through a new car, an experienced employee explaining a procedure to a new one, a parent renting party decorations. One clip of about 2 to 2.5 minutes, then about 6 questions. The register moves from informal to somewhat formal, and the questions chase details in the order the speaker gave them. Work through the Part 3 questions with a pen in your hand.

Part 4: Listening to a News Item

The first single speaker. It is a news report of about 1.5 minutes, formal and factual, like a radio bulletin: a local event where something unexpected happened, a new technology, an encounter with animals. About 5 questions follow. From this part onward the question format changes, which the next section explains, so treat Part 4 as a reset. The Part 4 question bank has news items in this shape.

Part 5: Listening to a Discussion

The only video. Three people talk for about 1.5 to 2 minutes, then you answer about 8 questions. They might be neighbours planning a block party or classmates dividing up a group project, and they will sometimes disagree. CELPIP says you are not tested on visual details that have nothing to do with the conversation, so ignore the shirt colours and watch faces instead: a raised eyebrow tells you who is unconvinced. Practise tracking three voices on the Part 5 questions.

Part 6: Listening for Viewpoints

One speaker, about 3 minutes, and it is the longest clip of the test. This is a prepared talk, not a chat: a speech on climate change, a presentation on a technology, a description of a community issue and the options for solving it. CELPIP warns that the speaker uses low-frequency and specialized language, which is why this part sits last. About 6 questions. If you only have time to practise one part before test day, make it Part 6.

How do the questions work in Parts 1 to 3 versus Parts 4 to 6?

This is the format detail most guides skip, and it changes how you take notes.

Parts 1 to 3 are heard. Each question is read to you, one at a time, with the four answer choices on screen. You get 30 seconds to hear and answer each one, and you must take them in order. You cannot skip ahead and come back. So in the first three parts your notes need to be in sequence, because the questions arrive in roughly the same order as the answers appeared in the audio.

Parts 4 to 6 are read. The questions are sentence completions. All of them appear on one screen, you read the question and the four choices yourself, and you get one block of time for the whole screen. You can answer in any order and change an answer as often as you like until that time runs out. Here the smart move is to answer what you are sure of first, then use what is left on the ones you are guessing.

In every part, each question has one correct answer out of four, and no points are taken off for a wrong one. Answer everything.

What are the three CELPIP Listening question types?

CELPIP sorts every Listening question into three types, and the same three appear on the Reading test. Once you can spot the type from the wording, you know where in your notes to look.

General Meaning. A big-picture question that asks you to pull together ideas from a long stretch of the clip or from more than one speaker. It sounds like "The two speakers are mainly discussing..." or "All three speakers agree that...". Look at your notes as a whole, not at one line.

Specific Information. A close-up question about one fact a speaker mentioned in one place: a name, a date, a price, a reason. It sounds like "The woman works as..." or "The man cannot come on Friday because...". This is where sequenced notes pay for themselves.

Inference. A question about something nobody said outright: how a speaker feels, what will probably happen next, what someone would most likely agree with. The giveaway words are "probably", "most likely", "sounds" and "feels". The answer is in the tone of voice and, in Part 5, in the faces.

Expect the correct option to be a paraphrase. The words on screen will not match the words in the audio, so a choice that repeats a phrase you heard is more often a distractor than the answer.

Is any part of CELPIP Listening unscored?

Yes, in two ways, and neither should cost you a minute of worry.

The first is the Practice Task. CELPIP's Listening Pro Study Pack lists it before Part 1 as one unscored question taking about a minute, and the 2025 Guidebook for Test Takers lists it as a Listening component. The July 2026 Guidebook does not mention it by name, so treat the exact placement as CELPIP's description rather than a promise. Its job is to let you check the headset and the volume.

The second is development content. The current Guidebook says the Listening and Reading tests may contain unscored content used for test development, that it can appear anywhere, and that it looks exactly like the scored content. You will not be able to tell which questions those are. CELPIP also says the time spent on that content is included in the total test time and does not take time away from scored tasks. The only sensible response is to treat every question as real.

Can you replay the audio or go back in CELPIP Listening?

No to both. Every clip plays one time only, it starts on its own, and there is no pause button. When a part's time ends, the test moves to the next screen and the previous part is locked.

What you do get is paper and a pen at the test centre, and you can ask for more paper. Your notes are never seen by anyone and have no effect on your score, so write badly and write fast. Two other things worth knowing before you sit down: the current CELPIP Guidebook describes the audio as a single North American accent, and in every two-person conversation one speaker is a woman and one is a man, which makes "the man said" and "the woman said" easy to track in your notes.

Good to know

Frequently Asked Questions

1

Is Listening the first section of the CELPIP test?

Yes. CELPIP states that Listening is the first part of the test, followed by Reading, Writing and Speaking. The full order and every section's timing is on our CELPIP test format guide.

2

Do the CELPIP Listening parts get harder?

Yes. CELPIP states that the audio passages and questions increase in difficulty from Part 1 to Part 6. Part 6 is the longest clip and uses the least common vocabulary.

3

How is the CELPIP Listening test scored?

Each correct answer is worth 1 point, nothing is deducted for a wrong answer, and the computer converts your points to a CELPIP level using the difficulty of the questions you were given. Our CELPIP Listening score chart shows how many correct answers each level takes.

4

What accent does CELPIP Listening use?

A single North American accent, in the words of the current CELPIP Guidebook for Test Takers, with everyday English and vocabulary. Two-speaker conversations always have one male and one female voice.
