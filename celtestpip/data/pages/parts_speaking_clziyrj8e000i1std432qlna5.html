Overview

Speaking - Part 5: Comparing and Persuading is a two-part task in the CELPIP Speaking Test where you first choose between two options and then persuade someone why your chosen option is better. This task assesses your ability to make comparisons, present logical arguments, and use persuasive language.

Structure

  • Part 1:

    • Time to Prepare: 60 seconds
    • Speaking Time: None (you only select an option)

  • Part 2:

    • Time to Prepare: 60 seconds
    • Speaking Time: 60 seconds

What to Expect

  • Part 1:

    • You will be presented with a scenario involving two options. Your task is to silently choose the option you prefer based on the information provided.

  • Part 2:

    • You will receive additional details about the option you did not choose. Your task is to explain why your choice is better and persuade the listener by comparing the two options.

Key Strategies

Part 1: Choosing an Option

  1. Read and Understand the Situation:

    • Quickly grasp the context of the scenario. What decision needs to be made? What are the key factors to consider?

  2. Compare the Options:

    • Analyze the advantages and disadvantages of both options. Consider aspects like cost, convenience, practicality, and personal preferences.

  3. Make a Quick Decision:

    • Decide which option you prefer based on the initial comparison. Remember, you don’t need to justify your choice in this part. Just make the decision.

Part 2: Persuading the Listener

  1. Analyze the Opposing Option:

    • After receiving more details about the option you did not choose, think about its potential weaknesses compared to your choice.

  2. Organize Your Arguments:

    • Structure your response with a clear introduction, body, and conclusion:
      • Introduction: State your chosen option and briefly mention the opposing option.
      • Body: Present 2-3 strong reasons why your choice is better, using comparisons and specific examples.
      • Conclusion: Summarize your points and reaffirm why your choice is superior.

  3. Use Persuasive Language:

    • Employ phrases that clearly indicate comparison and persuasion, such as "better than," "more suitable," "less expensive," "more practical," "easier to manage," etc.

  4. Stay Focused and Relevant:

    • Keep your arguments focused on the scenario. Avoid going off on tangents or discussing irrelevant details.

  5. Practice Fluency and Clarity:

    • Speak confidently and clearly, with minimal hesitation. Practice delivering your points within the 60-second time limit to ensure your response is both complete and persuasive.

Common Pitfalls

  • Overemphasizing Minor Details: Focus on the most compelling reasons rather than getting bogged down in less important aspects.
  • Unbalanced Comparison: Ensure you discuss both options fairly before concluding why your choice is better.
  • Lack of Specificity: Avoid vague statements. Use concrete examples and specific language to make your argument stronger.
  • Poor Time Management: Practice pacing your speech to cover all points effectively within the allotted time.

Final Advice

To excel in Speaking - Part 5: Comparing and Persuading, practice making quick decisions and articulating persuasive arguments clearly and concisely. Familiarize yourself with a variety of scenarios and options, and work on developing strong comparative and persuasive language skills. With regular practice, you’ll be able to deliver confident and convincing responses on test day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Speaking Task 5.

Do I have to speak in the first part of CELPIP Speaking Part 5?

No. In the first stage of CELPIP Speaking Part 5 you get 60 seconds to silently choose one of two options, with no speaking. You only speak in the second stage, where you persuade the listener why your choice is better.

How does the two-part structure of CELPIP Speaking Part 5 work?

CELPIP Speaking Part 5 (Comparing and Persuading) has two stages: first, review two options and pick one (60 seconds, silent); then you receive details about the option you did not pick and get 60 seconds to prepare and 60 seconds to argue why your choice wins.

How do I persuade effectively in CELPIP Speaking Task 5?

In CELPIP Speaking Task 5, give two or three reasons your choice beats the alternative, using specific comparisons like "more practical" or "less expensive." Acknowledge the other option fairly before explaining why yours is better, then reaffirm your choice.

Why do test-takers find CELPIP Speaking Part 5 tricky?

CELPIP Speaking Part 5 is tricky because it is the only two-stage speaking task and you must compare both options fairly before persuading. Focusing on minor details instead of your strongest reasons, or comparing unfairly, is where marks are lost.

What language should I use in CELPIP Speaking Part 5?

CELPIP Speaking Part 5 rewards comparative and persuasive phrases: "better than," "more suitable," "less expensive," "more practical," "easier to manage." For example: "Option A is more practical than Option B because it saves commute time," which makes your comparison explicit.

What kind of scenarios are in CELPIP Speaking Part 5?

CELPIP Speaking Part 5 presents everyday choices with trade-offs: two apartments, two products, two vacation plans, or two services, where each option has clear advantages and disadvantages you must weigh before choosing.

How do I improve at CELPIP Speaking Part 5?

To improve at CELPIP Speaking Part 5, practice choosing quickly between two options and defending your pick with fair, specific comparisons in 60 seconds. Rehearse acknowledging the other side first, then arguing why yours is stronger.
