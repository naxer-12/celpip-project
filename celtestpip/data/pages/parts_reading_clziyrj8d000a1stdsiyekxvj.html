What is CELPIP Reading Part 3 (Reading for Information)?

Reading Part 3, also called Reading Task 3, gives you a four-paragraph informational text about a person, place, event or process. You read 9 statements and decide which paragraph each one comes from, or choose E if the information is not in the text. You have about 10 minutes.

How many questions are in Reading Part 3, and how long do you get?

Part 3 has 9 questions in one set. CELPIP gives about 10 minutes for the whole part.

Each Reading part runs on its own clock. You can change your answers as often as you like while the time lasts. When it runs out, the test moves to the next part on its own and you cannot go back.

Every correct answer is worth 1 point and a wrong answer costs nothing, so answer every statement, even when you are unsure. Part 3 counts toward one CELPIP Reading level, based on all 38 scored questions. The CELPIP Reading score chart shows how many correct answers each level needs.

What will you read in Reading Part 3?

The text is formal, educational and descriptive. It gives facts, not opinions, and it is written for a general reader, not for experts on the subject.

CELPIP's examples include an explanation of how something is made or how it works, a description of an animal's territory and habits, a historical event or a popular landmark, and the story of someone's life.

How should you spend the 10 minutes?

CELPIP publishes a suggested plan for this part in its Reading study pack. It adds up to 10 minutes:

  1. Preview the text (1 minute). Find the topic of the reading and the main idea of each paragraph. Writing one or two words per paragraph on your paper makes the next step much faster.
  2. Match the statements (8.5 minutes). For each statement:
    • use your paragraph notes to find the paragraph it most likely belongs to;
    • scan that paragraph for keywords and for paraphrase of the statement;
    • read that part carefully to make sure the exact information in the statement is really there;
    • choose E if the information is not stated in the reading.
  3. Review (30 seconds). Make sure every statement has an answer.

For a pacing plan that covers all four parts, see our CELPIP Reading time guide.

Tips for CELPIP Reading Part 3

  • The whole statement must be there. If a paragraph says only half of it, that paragraph is not the answer. Check every part of the statement before you choose a letter.
  • True is not the same as stated. A statement can be true in real life and still be E, because the passage never says it. Answer from the text, not from what you know.
  • Same words can mislead, different words can be right. A paragraph that repeats a word from the statement may be about something else, while the correct paragraph often says the same thing in other words.
  • Rule out paragraphs. If you are unsure, cross out the paragraphs that clearly do not fit, then check the ones left against the text.
  • Keep moving. You have a little under a minute per statement. If one is slow, choose your best answer, note it on your paper and come back if time allows.

Common mistakes in Reading Part 3

  • Choosing a paragraph because it shares a word with the statement, not because it says the same thing.
  • Giving a paragraph letter to a statement that is only half there, when the answer is E.
  • Rereading the whole passage for every statement instead of using a paragraph map.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Reading Part 3.

What is the reading passage in CELPIP Reading Part 3 about?

A formal, factual text of four paragraphs about a person, place, event or process. CELPIP's examples include how something is made or works, an animal's territory and habits, a historical event or landmark, and someone's life story. It informs; it does not argue for an opinion.

How do I find the answers quickly in CELPIP Reading Part 3?

Spend the first minute mapping the text: one or two words for what each paragraph covers. Then, for each statement, use its keywords to go to the likely paragraph, and read only that part closely. Answers are paraphrased, so match the idea, not the wording.

How many questions and how long is CELPIP Reading for Information?

9 questions in about 10 minutes. CELPIP's suggested plan is 1 minute to preview the topic and each paragraph's main idea, 8.5 minutes to match the statements, and 30 seconds to make sure every question has an answer.

How is CELPIP Reading Part 3 different from Part 1?

Part 1 is an everyday email and a reply with blanks. Part 3 is one formal, factual passage of four paragraphs, with no reply. Instead of filling blanks, you match each statement to the paragraph that contains it, or choose E when the passage does not say it.

What reading skill does CELPIP Reading Part 3 test?

Skimming and scanning: finding specific information in a longer factual text without reading every word. It also tests paraphrase, because statements say the same thing as the passage in different words, and judgement, because some statements are not in the text at all.

How do I match statements to the right paragraph in CELPIP Reading Part 3?

Pick out the statement's keywords and go to the paragraph whose main idea fits. Check that the whole statement is there, not only part of it. If no paragraph says it, choose E. A statement can be true in real life and still be E.

How can I improve my CELPIP Reading Part 3 score?

Practise skimming and scanning on short factual articles: map the paragraphs first, then find details. Train yourself to spot paraphrase, and be strict about E: if the text does not say it, it is not there. Timed sets of 9 statements in about 10 minutes build the pace.
