CELPIP Writing Samples with Answers: Task 1 and 2 (2026)

CELPIP Writing has two tasks and 53 minutes in total. Task 1 is an email in 27 minutes and Task 2 is a survey response in 26 minutes, both 150 to 200 words. Below are four sample answers: two Task 1 emails and two Task 2 survey responses, each labelled with its level and the reason.

Every prompt on this page was written by us, and every answer is our own example. Read one sample, then write your own answer to the same prompt with a 27 or 26 minute timer running. Reading a good answer shows you the target. Writing your own is what moves the score. When you are ready, write a Task 1 or Task 2 answer and get an AI score.

What are the two CELPIP Writing tasks?

Two tasks, one after the other, typed on the computer. Each task has its own time limit: 27 minutes for Task 1, then 26 minutes for Task 2.

TaskWhat you writeTimeLength
Task 1: Writing an EmailAn email about an everyday situation, usually covering about three points the prompt lists27 minutes150 to 200 words
Task 2: Responding to Survey QuestionsYour choice between two options, with reasons26 minutes150 to 200 words

The screen gives you a timer, a word counter and a spell check. The spell check only flags spelling, and you pick each correction yourself, so it will not catch a wrong word that is spelled correctly.

How are these sample answers labelled?

Each sample is our example of a Level 9 or Level 10 answer. CELPIP did not rate any of them. Only trained raters can give you a real level, so no website can promise that an answer "scores 10". What we can do is write each answer to what CELPIP's published Writing level descriptors describe, and show you where that happens.

CELPIP rates Writing on four dimensions: Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability and Task Fulfillment. Under each sample you will find one short note per dimension. CELPIP levels match CLB (Canadian Language Benchmark) levels one to one, so a Level 9 here means CLB 9 on an immigration file. What each level means for Express Entry is on the CELPIP Writing score chart.

Task 1 sample 1: formal email to a business (Level 10 example)

Prompt (written by us): Two weeks ago you bought a used car from a local dealership. Five days later, the air conditioning stopped working. Write an email to the dealership manager. In your email:

  • Describe the problem.
  • Explain how it has affected you.
  • Say what you would like the dealership to do.

Sample answer (172 words):

Dear Manager,

I am writing about the 2019 Honda Civic I bought from your dealership on November 3. Five days after I drove it home, the air conditioning stopped working completely. The fan still runs, but it only blows warm air, even on the coldest setting.

This has caused me real difficulty. I drive 45 minutes to work each way, and with the windows closed the car becomes uncomfortably hot by mid afternoon. Last Thursday I had to stop twice on the highway because my young son, who was in the back seat, felt unwell from the heat.

When I bought the car, your salesperson told me it had passed a full inspection, and the receipt lists the air conditioning as checked. I would therefore like you to repair the system at no cost to me within the next week. If that is not possible, I would ask for a loaner car until the repair is done.

Thank you for your attention. I look forward to hearing from you.

Sincerely,
Daniel Okafor

Why this is our Level 10 example:

  • Content/Coherence: every point has a specific detail (a date, a 45 minute drive, the inspection receipt), and each paragraph handles one point of the prompt in order.
  • Vocabulary: precise everyday words ("loaner car", "at no cost to me") instead of fancy ones.
  • Readability: sentences vary in length, and linking words ("therefore", "if that is not possible") carry the logic.
  • Task Fulfillment: all three points answered, a clear request with a deadline, and a polite formal tone for a manager the writer has never met.

Task 1 sample 2: informal email to a friend (Level 9 example)

Prompt (written by us): A friend from your old neighbourhood is moving to your city next month and has asked you for advice. Write an email to your friend. In your email:

  • Recommend a good area to live.
  • Explain the best way to get around the city.
  • Suggest something to do on the first weekend.

Sample answer (166 words):

Hi Priya,

I'm so happy you're finally moving here! You asked where to live, and my honest answer is the east side, near Riverside Park. Rent is a bit lower than downtown, the streets are quiet at night, and there are two big grocery stores within walking distance.

Getting around is easier than you think. The light rail runs every ten minutes from Riverside station to the city centre, and a monthly pass is much cheaper than paying for parking. I sold my car last year and I honestly don't miss it. Just download the transit app before you arrive so you can check the times.

For your first weekend, come with me to the Saturday farmers' market by the river. It's a great place to try local food, and you'll get a feel for the neighbourhood at the same time. After that we can grab coffee and I'll show you around.

Let me know your moving date and I'll help you unpack!

Talk soon,
Sam

Why this is our Level 9 example:

  • Content/Coherence: each of the three points gets its own paragraph and at least one reason or detail.
  • Vocabulary: natural informal phrases ("grab coffee", "a feel for the neighbourhood") that fit a friend.
  • Readability: contractions and a friendly rhythm, with almost no errors.
  • Task Fulfillment: all three points covered in the right tone. What would lift it to a 10: one more concrete detail per point, for example the actual rent range or the name of the market.

CELPIP Writing Task 2 samples with answers

Task 2 is a survey. You read a short situation, choose Option A or Option B, and explain why. You do not need a greeting or a sign-off. Open with your choice, give two reasons with examples, and say briefly why the other option is weaker.

Task 2 sample 1: newcomer classes or a sports field (Level 10 example)

Prompt (written by us): Your city council has extra money in this year's budget and is asking residents how to spend it. Choose the option you prefer and explain why.

  • Option A: Offer free evening English classes for newcomers.
  • Option B: Build a new community sports field.

Sample answer (167 words):

I would choose free evening English classes for newcomers, because they help people build a stable life in the city, and that benefit spreads to everyone around them.

First, language is often the biggest barrier to a good job. Many newcomers arrive with real skills, such as nursing or engineering, but cannot pass an interview in English. Evening classes let them study after work, so they can move into better paid jobs and pay more in local taxes over time.

Second, the classes would bring people together. When my neighbour joined a similar program, she met families from her area, and now she volunteers at the school where our children study. Classes like these turn strangers into active residents.

A new sports field would certainly be enjoyable, and young people need places to play. However, our city already has several parks with open fields, while free adult English classes are hard to find. For these reasons, I believe Option A is the better use of the money.

Why this is our Level 10 example:

  • Content/Coherence: a clear choice in the first sentence, two reasons that each have a concrete example, and a final paragraph that weighs the other option.
  • Vocabulary: exact words ("barrier", "active residents") without overreaching.
  • Readability: a mix of short and long sentences, and the logic is easy to follow.
  • Task Fulfillment: a firm position, held to the end, written as a resident answering a survey rather than as an academic essay.

Task 2 sample 2: school gym after hours (Level 9 example)

Prompt (written by us): Your local school is deciding how to use its gym in the evenings. Choose the option you prefer and explain why.

  • Option A: Open the gym to adults for evening fitness classes.
  • Option B: Keep the gym for youth sports programs.

Sample answer (165 words):

In my opinion, the school should keep the gym for youth sports programs. I think this option helps the community more for two main reasons.

First, teenagers need a safe place to go after school. In my area, many parents work until six or seven, so some kids spend the evening alone or on the street. A basketball or volleyball program in the gym would give them something healthy to do with adults watching them.

Second, youth sports teach skills that last. When I played on a team in high school, I learned how to work with others and manage my time, and these skills helped me later at work. Coaches can also notice when a student is having problems at home and help them find support.

I understand that adults also want to stay fit, and evening classes would be nice. However, adults have more choices, like community centres and private gyms, while children have very few. For these reasons, I prefer Option B.

Why this is our Level 9 example:

  • Content/Coherence: a clear choice and two reasons, each with a personal example.
  • Vocabulary: correct and clear, but mostly common words ("nice", "something healthy to do").
  • Readability: easy to read and almost error free, with simple linking ("First", "Second", "However").
  • Task Fulfillment: answers the survey and mentions the other option. What would lift it to a 10: more precise vocabulary and one more specific detail, such as how many students would join or which nearby gyms adults already use.

What separates a Level 7 answer from a Level 10?

Usually not the idea. It is the detail, the control and the tone. Here is how we teach the difference, dimension by dimension:

DimensionTypical Level 7 answerTypical Level 10 answer
Content/CoherenceCovers the points, with general support ("it was very bad for me")Every point backed by a specific detail: a date, a number, a real example
VocabularyCommon words, some repeated ("very", "good")Precise words that fit the situation, without showing off
ReadabilitySome errors that do not block meaning; simple sentence patternsFew errors; sentence length varies; linking words carry the logic
Task FulfillmentAnswers the prompt, but the tone may slip or a point may be thinEvery point answered in a tone that fits the reader from start to finish

The quickest way to move up is the first row. Go back to your last practice answer and replace every general sentence with a specific one. That single change often lifts an answer by a level.

Should I memorise a sample answer?

No. Memorise the structure, not the words. Test day prompts are different from any sample, and an answer that does not fit the prompt loses marks on Task Fulfillment however polished it sounds. Use the samples to learn the shape: what goes in each paragraph, how much detail each point needs, how to open and close. We explain the line between a useful frame and a risky script in memorised templates on CELPIP.

Then practise on real prompts: Task 1 email questions and Task 2 survey questions. For the habits that get answers to 9 and above, read our CELPIP Writing tips.

Good to know

Frequently Asked Questions

1

How many words should a CELPIP Writing answer be?

Between 150 and 200 words for each task. There is some room to go a little under or over, but an answer far outside the range can lose marks. The word counter on screen shows your count as you type.

2

How is CELPIP Writing scored?

Trained raters score each answer on four dimensions: Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability and Task Fulfillment. The result is a CELPIP level, and from Level 3 up it matches the same CLB level.

3

Is there a spell check in the CELPIP Writing test?

Yes. It flags spelling mistakes and lets you choose a correction. There is no auto-correct and no grammar check, so a correctly spelled wrong word, like "affect" for "effect", will not be flagged.

4

Where can I practise CELPIP Writing with feedback?

On CELTESTPIP Writing practice. After you sign up, the first question of each task is free, and you can submit it for AI feedback up to three times per task. The feedback is CELTESTPIP's own estimate, not a CELPIP score.
