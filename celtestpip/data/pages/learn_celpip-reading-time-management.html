CELPIP Reading Time Management: Minutes per Part (2026)

CELPIP Reading gives about 11, 9, 10 and 13 minutes per part, each on its own clock, and you cannot go back. Here is how to pace all four and finish.

CELPIP Reading gives you 43 to 56 minutes for 38 scored questions in 4 parts, and each part runs on its own clock: about 11, 9, 10 and 13 minutes. When a part's time ends, the test moves you on and you cannot go back. So time management means finishing each part inside its own limit.

That one fact changes how you should practise. In my experience, most people who miss their Reading target did not run out of English; they ran out of time in one part and carried the panic into the next. Below is the official timing for each part, where the minutes actually go, and the pacing I teach for Parts 1 to 4.

Every part has a free sample test on the real clock. Start a free CELPIP Reading practice test, and time yourself before you read any further.

How much time do you get in CELPIP Reading?

About 43 minutes of scored reading, inside a section that CELPIP times at 43 to 56 minutes. CELPIP's Reading Pro Study Pack gives this breakdown, and labels both columns "approximate":

PartQuestions (approximate)Time (approximate)
Part 1: Reading Correspondence1111 minutes
Part 2: Reading to Apply a Diagram89 minutes
Part 3: Reading for Information910 minutes
Part 4: Reading for Viewpoints1013 minutes
Sum of the four parts (our total, not printed by CELPIP)3843 minutes

Why does the official Guidebook say 43 to 56 minutes for the section? Because the Reading test can contain unscored questions used for test development. CELPIP's study pack says there may be one unscored part, that you will not be told which one it is, and that you should do your best on every part. An extra part is where the other 13 minutes can come from. You will not be told which part it was, so treat every part as if it counts.

Two more official details matter for pacing. Each correct answer is worth one point, and points are not deducted for wrong answers, so a guess is always better than a blank. And the passages and questions get harder from Part 1 to Part 4, which is why Part 4 gets the most time.

Why can't you carry time from one part to the next?

Because each part has its own timer, and the test moves you forward when it runs out. CELPIP's study pack explains the rule. Inside a Reading part, you can change any answer as often as you want until the time runs out. Then the test moves you to the next part on its own, and there is no way back.

In practice that means three things:

  • Finishing Part 1 in 8 minutes does not give you 3 extra minutes for Part 4. Spare time in a part is only useful for checking that part.
  • A question you leave blank in Part 2 is gone once Part 3 starts. Answer everything before the clock ends, even if some answers are guesses.
  • The text and all its questions sit on one screen, and you scroll between them. You get paper and a pen for notes, but you cannot highlight or write on the screen.

So the skill is not "manage 50 minutes". It is "manage 11 minutes, then 9, then 10, then 13", four times in a row, with no rollover.

Where does the time actually go?

Usually into reading the passage twice and getting stuck on one question. In my experience, these are the four leaks:

  1. Reading the whole passage carefully before looking at a single question. You then read it again to find each answer.
  2. Re-reading a question three times because the options look alike. CELPIP distractors are built to look right.
  3. Spending two minutes on one hard question and rushing the last four easy ones.
  4. Checking answers you were already sure about instead of the two you doubted.

Every one of these is a habit, which is good news, because habits change with a few timed sets. The fixes are below, part by part.

How should you pace Part 1: Reading Correspondence (about 11 minutes)?

Preview the first question set, read the email once, answer, then handle the reply. Part 1 is an email from one community member to another about an everyday social or work situation, with questions in two groups: questions on the email, then a short response with gaps to fill in. My split for the 11 minutes:

MinuteWhat you do
0 to 1Skim the first question set so you know what to look for
1 to 4Read the email once, properly, noting who is writing to whom and why
4 to 7Answer the first set, going back to the exact line for each
7 to 10Read the reply and fill the gaps, using the original email to check facts
10 to 11Check only the answers you were unsure about

The reply gaps are the trap. They test whether you understood the first email, so each gap is answered by something in the original message, not by what sounds polite. Our Reading Part 1 vocabulary and tone guide covers the formal and informal wording these emails use.

How should you pace Part 2: Reading to Apply a Diagram (about 9 minutes)?

Read the diagram first, then the message, then answer the gaps from the diagram. Part 2 gives you a diagram with point-form text and visuals (a transit schedule, an event flyer, a guide to a community service) and a short email about it: five gaps to fill in, then three more questions. Nine minutes for 8 questions sounds generous and is not, because people read the message twice.

MinuteWhat you do
0 to 2Study the diagram: headings, numbers, dates, prices, labels
2 to 4Read the message once and underline, on your paper, every detail it takes from the diagram
4 to 7Fill the five gaps in the email, checking each one against the diagram, not the message
7 to 8Answer the last three questions
8 to 9Check the two you doubted

Most wrong answers in Part 2 come from choosing the option that fits the sentence grammatically instead of the one that matches the diagram. The diagram is the answer key.

How should you pace Part 3: Reading for Information (about 10 minutes)?

Read the questions first, then read the text one paragraph at a time and answer as you go. Part 3 is a four-paragraph informational text about a person, place, event or process. Each question is a statement you match to the paragraph that contains it, or to the "not stated" option if no paragraph does. Ten minutes for 9 questions.

MinuteWhat you do
0 to 1Read all the statements, and circle the keywords on paper
1 to 6Read the text one paragraph at a time; after each paragraph, match any statement it contains
6 to 9Go back for the statements still open, scanning for their keywords; if a statement is in no paragraph, choose "not stated"
9 to 10Check the two you doubted

The time leak in Part 3 is searching the whole text for every statement, and searching twice for one that is not there. Give any statement one careful search, then decide.

How should you pace Part 4: Reading for Viewpoints (about 13 minutes)?

Split the time: about 7 minutes for the article and its questions, about 6 for the comment. Part 4 is the hardest text: an article with two or more opinions about a community issue, followed by a short reader comment with gaps to fill in. Ten questions, split five and five.

MinuteWhat you do
0 to 1Skim the first five questions: they tell you whose opinion to track
1 to 5Read the article, noting on paper which person holds which view
5 to 7Answer the first five
7 to 11Read the comment and fill its gaps, checking each against the article's views
11 to 13Check the ones you doubted, and make sure nothing is blank

Part 4 is where people arrive tired and behind, which is exactly why it has the most time. If you have paced Parts 1 to 3, you walk into Part 4 with your full 13 minutes and a clear head. If you want the strategy for the opinion-matching questions themselves, the CELPIP Reading tips and strategies guide goes deeper on each part.

What do you do when you are running out of time?

Answer every question, then use what is left on the ones you doubted. Three rules I give every student:

  • The 60-second rule. If you have spent a minute on a question and you are no closer, choose your best option, note the number on your paper, and move on. Come back only if the part's clock allows it.
  • Never leave a blank. Wrong answers cost nothing, so a guess with a 25 percent chance beats a blank with none. In the last 30 seconds of any part, fill every gap.
  • Watch the clock at the halfway point, not the end. In an 11-minute part, if you are not halfway through the questions at minute 6, speed up now, not at minute 10.

And one rule for after the part ends: let it go. You cannot return to it, and the next part has its own clock. Thinking about a Part 2 question during Part 3 costs you Part 3.

How do you practise CELPIP Reading timing?

On the real clock, one part at a time, then all four in a row. Untimed reading practice improves your English but not your pacing. Build it in three steps:

  1. One part, timed. Sign up for a free CELTESTPIP account and take the free sample test in each Reading part on its real clock, as many times as you need. Note where the time went.
  2. Fix the leak, then repeat. If you ran out of time in Part 3, practise Part 3 with the preview method above until you finish with a minute to spare.
  3. All four in order. Once each part is under control, sit a full Reading section in one go, Part 1 to Part 4, so you learn what tired feels like in Part 4. Full sections are part of the CELPIP mock exams.

🎥 Take a full timed Reading test, then check every answer (12:30)

All 4 parts and 38 questions in one sitting. Pause at each test page, set your timer to that part's clock (11, 9, 10 and 13 minutes), answer on paper, then check every answer against the line in the text that proves it. This is the last step above: all four parts in a row, each on its own clock.

Good to know

Frequently Asked Questions

1

Is there a time limit for each part of CELPIP Reading?

Yes. Each part has its own clock, and CELPIP's Reading study pack gives approximate times of 11, 9, 10 and 13 minutes for Parts 1 to 4. When the time for a part ends, the test moves you to the next part automatically.

2

Can you go back to a previous part in CELPIP Reading?

No. CELPIP's study pack is explicit: when your time is up, the test moves forward, and you cannot go back to a previous part. Within a part, you can change your answers as often as you like until the clock ends.

3

How many questions are on the CELPIP Reading test?

38 scored questions: 11 in Part 1, 8 in Part 2, 9 in Part 3 and 10 in Part 4. There may also be one unscored part, and you will not know which one it is.

4

Is the CELPIP Reading test 43 to 56 minutes or 55 to 60 minutes?

The current CELPIP Guidebook for Test Takers (July 2026) gives 43 to 56 minutes. One line in the Reading study pack still says about 55 to 60. Plan for the parts, not the total: about 43 minutes of scored reading, plus a possible unscored part.

5

Do you lose points for wrong answers in CELPIP Reading?

No. Each correct answer earns one point and nothing is deducted for a wrong one, so you should answer every question before a part's clock runs out.
