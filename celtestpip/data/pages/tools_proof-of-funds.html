One person needs $15,263 to immigrate through Express Entry, and a family of four needs $28,362. That is IRCC's minimum in the table updated July 7, 2025, still the current one. It applies to the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs. The Canadian Experience Class needs none, and neither do you if you can already work in Canada and have a valid job offer.

The money is not a fee. You keep it: IRCC only wants proof that you can support your family while you settle. Use the calculator above for your own number, then read what counts as proof.

A family of nine, for example, needs $40,392 plus 2 × $4,112: $48,616. If you have more than the minimum, IRCC asks you to enter the full amount in your profile, not just the minimum.

Who needs proof of funds for Express Entry?

Candidates invited under the Federal Skilled Worker Program or the Federal Skilled Trades Program. Two groups are exempt:

  • The Canadian Experience Class. It has no proof of funds requirement at all.
  • Anyone authorized to work in Canada who also has a valid job offer, even under FSW or FST. You need both at the same time: a work permit (or other authorization) and the offer.

There is a catch. Your profile can qualify for more than one program, and you only learn which one when you are invited. IRCC asks you to keep your funds up to date in your profile for that reason. If you are in Canada on a work permit but have no job offer, plan for the funds: an FSW invitation would need them.

The system also asks every applicant to upload a proof of funds document. If you are exempt, upload a letter that explains why: you were invited under the Canadian Experience Class, or you have a valid job offer and are authorized to work in Canada.

Not sure which program you qualify for? The Express Entry eligibility checker tests you against all three.

How do you count your family size?

Count yourself, your spouse or common-law partner, your dependent children and your partner's dependent children. Count them even if they are Canadian citizens or permanent residents, and even if they are not coming to Canada with you.

That second rule surprises people. A spouse who stays behind still makes you a family of two, so the minimum moves from $15,263 to $19,001.

What documents prove your funds?

Official letters from every bank or financial institution where you hold an account. Each letter must be on the institution's letterhead and show:

  • the institution's contact details: address, phone number and email
  • your name
  • your outstanding debts, such as credit cards and loans
  • for each current bank and investment account: the account number, the date it was opened, the current balance and the average balance for the past 6 months

Source: IRCC, Proof of funds, "What we accept as proof".

What money counts as proof of funds?

Money you can legally use in Canada when you arrive. IRCC gives two examples of what does not count: the equity in a house or other property, and money borrowed from another person.

  • Joint accounts with a spouse who is coming with you count.
  • An account in your spouse's name only may count, but you must prove you can access the money.
  • The money must cover your whole family, including family members who are not coming with you.

The funds must be available both when you apply and when IRCC issues your permanent resident visa. A big balance that disappears before your visa is issued is a problem.

Is proof of funds the same as the fees you pay?

No. Proof of funds is your own money that you keep. The government fees are separate: $1,590 for you and the same for a spouse or partner, $270 for each dependent child, plus biometrics. The Express Entry cost calculator adds them up for your family.

Proof of funds gives no Comprehensive Ranking System points either. Your rank in the pool comes from your CRS score, where language tests carry the most weight.

Frequently asked questions

How much proof of funds do I need for Express Entry?

$15,263 for one person, $19,001 for two, $28,362 for four. IRCC's table (updated July 7, 2025) goes up to $40,392 for seven family members and adds $4,112 for each member after that.

Do I need proof of funds for the Canadian Experience Class?

No. The Canadian Experience Class has no proof of funds requirement. You still upload a letter explaining that you were invited under it, because the system asks every applicant for a proof of funds document.

Do I need proof of funds if I have a job offer?

Not if you are also authorized to work in Canada. IRCC exempts candidates who can work in Canada and have a valid job offer, even under the Federal Skilled Worker or Federal Skilled Trades program. A job offer while you live outside Canada without a work permit does not exempt you.

Does my spouse count if they are not coming to Canada?

Yes. IRCC counts your spouse or common-law partner and all dependent children in your family size, even if they are not coming with you or are already Canadian citizens or permanent residents.

Can I borrow money or use my house for proof of funds?

No. IRCC says you cannot borrow the money from another person and cannot use the equity in real property. The money must be yours, available to you, and usable for your family's living costs in Canada.

How long do the funds need to be in my account?

IRCC asks for the average balance over the past 6 months. Your bank letter must show each account's current balance and its 6-month average, and the money must still be there when you apply and when your permanent resident visa is issued.

Your language score matters more than your bank balance

Proof of funds only gets you through the door. Your rank in the Express Entry pool comes from CRS points, and language is the biggest part of them: CLB 9 in all four abilities is worth far more than any amount of money.
