How Long Does It Take to Prepare for CELPIP? 4-Week Plan

Most people need about 4 weeks at 10 to 15 hours a week. Find how long you need for your level, then follow this day-by-day 4-week CELPIP study plan.

Most people need about four weeks to prepare for CELPIP, studying 10 to 15 hours a week, if their English is already around Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 6 or 7. Starting below CLB 5, plan on 12 weeks or more. Already at CLB 8 and aiming for 9? Two to three focused weeks is often enough.

That is the honest range I give my own students. The rest of this page does two jobs: it helps you work out which group you are in, and then it gives you the day-by-day plan for the four weeks most people need. If your test is closer than that, the last-minute CELPIP study plan is built for days, not weeks.

How long does it take to prepare for CELPIP?

It depends on two numbers: where your English is today and the level your program asks for. The bigger the gap, the longer you need. Here is how I would plan it, at 10 to 15 hours a week:

Your level nowYour targetPlan forHow to use this page
CLB 8 or higher9 or more2 to 3 weeksSqueeze Week 1 into two or three days, then do Weeks 2 to 4
CLB 6 to 77 to 9about 4 weeks (one month)Follow the plan as written
CLB 5 to 676 to 8 weeksDo the plan, repeating Weeks 2 and 3
Below CLB 5712 weeks or moreBuild everyday English first, then start Week 1

These are teaching estimates, not official figures. CELPIP does not publish a recommended preparation time, and nobody can promise a level by a date. What the table gives you is a realistic starting point, so you can book a test date you can actually be ready for.

Not sure where you are? Spend one evening finding out. Do one timed practice part in each skill: CELPIP's own free online sample test works, or sign up for a free CELTESTPIP account and answer the first question in every part and task, all 20 of them, with feedback on each. Your weakest skill tells you more than your average does, because most programs set a minimum in every skill.

How do you prepare for CELPIP?

You prepare in five steps, in this order. Each one is a week (or part of one) in the plan below.

  1. Learn the format. Know every part, every timer and every question type before you study a single strategy. (Week 1)
  2. Take a cold baseline. Do one practice test per skill with no preparation, so you know your real starting point. (Week 1)
  3. Learn the strategies. One skill per day, applied straight away to practice questions. (Week 2)
  4. Practise under the clock. Timed sets, with extra sessions on your two weakest skills. (Week 3)
  5. Sit a full mock, then review. Back to back, timed, followed by a light final week so you arrive rested. (Weeks 2 to 4)

If you would rather learn each skill in order with a teacher, every lesson in the free CELTESTPIP courses is free, and they fit alongside this plan.

CELPIP Study Plan Week 1: Learn the Test Format

Goal: understand every section of the test, find your strengths and weaknesses, and set up your daily study habit.

Day 1 (Monday): Test Format Deep Dive

Essential tasks:

  • Read the CELPIP test format guide carefully. Understand every part of every section.
  • Take notes on the number of questions, the time limits and the question types for each section.
  • Watch two or three CELPIP walkthrough videos to see what the test screen looks like.

Why this matters: many test takers lose points simply because they do not know the format. Knowing that the Listening audio plays only once changes how you listen from the very first clip.

Day 2 (Tuesday): First Exposure to Listening

Essential tasks:

  • Take a full Listening practice test. CELPIP's free online sample test works, or use a full Listening set in CELTESTPIP Listening practice.
  • Do not study strategies yet. Take it cold to see where you stand.
  • When you finish, review every wrong answer and note which parts were hardest.

Day 3 (Wednesday): First Exposure to Reading

Essential tasks:

  • Take a full Reading practice test in CELTESTPIP Reading practice, timed.
  • Again, no strategies. Just your raw performance.
  • Review wrong answers and track which parts cost you the most points.

Day 4 (Thursday): First Exposure to Writing

Essential tasks:

  • Write one Task 1 email (27 minutes) and one Task 2 survey response (26 minutes), timed.
  • Use CELTESTPIP Writing practice. With a free account you can submit the first prompt in each task up to three times, with AI feedback each time.
  • When you finish, check your work: did you cover every point the prompt asked for? Is your tone right for the reader? Did you finish on time?

Key things to notice:

  • Did you run out of time?
  • Did you miss any of the points in the Task 1 prompt?
  • Did you pick a clear side in Task 2?
  • How long did you spend planning compared with writing?

Day 5 (Friday): First Exposure to Speaking

Essential tasks:

  • Record yourself answering all 8 Speaking tasks in CELTESTPIP Speaking practice. With a free account you can answer the first question in every task up to three times, with feedback each time.
  • Play your recordings back and listen critically. Do you use filler words? Do you fill the full time? Is your pace natural?
  • Note which tasks felt easiest and which felt hardest.

Days 6 and 7 (Weekend): Review and Plan

Essential tasks:

  • Review your results from Days 2 to 5. Find your two weakest sections: they get extra time in Weeks 2 and 3.
  • Read the 15 common CELPIP mistakes and mark the ones that apply to you.
  • Write your Week 2 schedule, adjusting the plan below around your weak spots.

Milestone check: by the end of Week 1 you should know what each section looks like, where your weak areas are, and roughly what level you are starting from.

CELPIP Study Plan Week 2: Learn the Strategies

Goal: learn specific strategies for each section and start using them in practice.

Day 8 (Monday): Listening Strategies

Essential tasks:

  • Read our CELPIP Listening tips.
  • Practise note taking: listen to a three-minute CBC podcast clip, write point-form notes, then check how many key facts you caught.
  • Redo two Listening parts using the preview-and-note approach and compare with Day 2.

Focus on:

  • Reading the on-screen context before the audio plays (the questions come after the clip).
  • Writing keywords only on your note paper, not sentences.
  • Letting go of a missed question instead of getting stuck on it.

Day 9 (Tuesday): Reading Strategies

Essential tasks:

  • Read our CELPIP Reading tips.
  • Practise skim-then-scan on two Reading parts.
  • Time yourself per question and aim for under 60 seconds each.

Focus on:

  • Building a mental map of the passage before you answer.
  • The 60-second rule for tough questions.
  • Labelling paragraphs in Part 3.

Day 10 (Wednesday): Writing Strategies

Essential tasks:

  • Learn how CELPIP Writing is rated: Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability and Task Fulfillment. Grammar is judged inside Readability, not as a separate score.
  • Practise Task 1 with a planning step: spend three or four minutes outlining before you write.
  • Study our Writing Task 1 template and Writing Task 2 template for structure.

Focus on:

  • Matching your tone to the reader: formal for a manager or a company, informal for a friend.
  • Covering every point the Task 1 prompt asks for.
  • Taking a clear position in Task 2.

Day 11 (Thursday): Speaking Strategies

Essential tasks:

  • Review all 8 Speaking task types and what each one asks for.
  • Practise Tasks 1, 2 and 3 with the 30 seconds of preparation time. Use it to organise your ideas, not to script every word.
  • Record yourself and listen back, cutting filler words ("um", "uh", "like").

Focus on:

  • Using the full speaking time instead of stopping early.
  • Speaking at a natural, steady pace.
  • Matching your tone to the situation in the prompt.

Day 12 (Friday): Weak Section Deep Dive

Essential tasks:

  • Spend the whole session on your weakest section from Week 1.
  • Do three or four practice parts for it, using the strategies.
  • Review every mistake and write down the specific fix.

Days 13 and 14 (Weekend): Full Practice Test #1

Essential tasks:

  • Take a complete CELPIP mock test with all four sections, back to back and timed.
  • Copy test conditions: no phone, no pauses, no looking things up.

After the test:

  • Score each section.
  • Compare with your Week 1 baseline and look for improvement.
  • Note any strategies you forgot to use under pressure.

Milestone check: by the end of Week 2 you should be comfortable with a strategy for every section and see measurable improvement on your Week 1 baseline.

CELPIP Study Plan Week 3: Intensive Practice

Goal: use your strategies under pressure, build consistency, and close the gaps in your weak areas.

Day 15 (Monday): Listening Intensive

  • Do three full Listening parts back to back, timed.
  • Focus on Parts 5 and 6, the hardest parts for most people.
  • Practise tracking several speakers' viewpoints in your notes.
  • Review wrong answers: was it a detail you missed, or a viewpoint you mixed up?

Day 16 (Tuesday): Reading Intensive

  • Do three full Reading parts back to back, timed.
  • Focus on Part 3 (paragraph matching) and Part 4 (viewpoints).
  • Practise spotting paraphrase: when you find the right answer, note how the passage and the question say the same thing in different words.
  • Track your time per question.

Day 17 (Wednesday): Writing Intensive

  • Write two complete Task 1 emails and two complete Task 2 responses, each timed.
  • Check each one against the four rating areas: did you cover every point, is the tone right, are the paragraphs clear, and are there errors you could have caught?
  • Practise the last two or three minutes: finish writing, then proofread for your usual errors.

Day 18 (Thursday): Speaking Intensive

  • Practise all 8 tasks with new prompts.
  • Record every answer and review it.
  • Do your two weakest tasks three times each, with different topics.
  • Give extra time to Task 5 (Comparing and Persuading) and Task 6 (Dealing with a Difficult Situation). They need the most structure.

Day 19 (Friday): Weak Section Round 2

  • Go back to your weakest section for another deep dive.
  • Compare with your Day 12 session and look for improvement.
  • If one part still beats you, drill that part on its own.

Days 20 and 21 (Weekend): Full Practice Test #2

  • Take another full practice test under real conditions.
  • This time, watch your time management. Are you finishing each section with time to spare, or running out?
  • Score it and compare with Practice Test #1.

CELPIP Study Plan Week 4: Final Review and Test Day

Goal: fine-tune, build confidence, and peak on test day.

Day 22 (Monday): Light Full Review

  • Do one practice part from each section (Listening Part 5, Reading Part 4, one Writing task, two Speaking tasks).
  • Quality over quantity: apply every strategy cleanly.
  • Review any weak points that are left.

Day 23 (Tuesday): Writing and Speaking Focus

  • Write one final Task 1 and one Task 2, timed.
  • Practise your four weakest Speaking tasks.
  • This is your last heavy practice day, so give it full energy.

Day 24 (Wednesday): Listening and Reading Focus

  • Do two Listening parts and two Reading parts.
  • Focus on pacing and the 60-second rule.
  • If you are hitting your target, keep doing what you are doing.

Day 25 (Thursday): Final Practice Test (Optional)

  • If you feel you need it, take one more practice test.
  • If you feel ready, skip it and do light review only: reread your strategies and your past mistakes.
  • Do not cram new material. Reinforcing what you know is worth more now than learning something new.

Day 26 (Friday): Test Eve Preparation

Essential tasks:

  • Read our CELPIP test day checklist.
  • Lay out your ID and your confirmation email.
  • Look up your test centre and plan your route.
  • Set two alarms.
  • Go to bed at a sensible time. Sleep matters more than any last-minute studying.

Do not:

  • Take a practice test the night before.
  • Try to learn new strategies or vocabulary.
  • Stay up late cramming.
  • Change your approach or try something new.

Days 27 and 28 (Weekend): Test Day and Recovery

Test day:

  • Arrive 45 minutes before your start time.
  • Stay calm. You have prepared for four weeks and you know what to expect.
  • In Speaking, use the unscored practice task to settle your voice before the scored tasks begin.
  • Trust your preparation and apply your strategies.

After the test:

  • Your scores usually appear in your CELPIP account within a few business days, and you get an email when they are ready.
  • Do not second-guess yourself. What is done is done.

Daily CELPIP Study Habits for All 4 Weeks

These small daily habits add up over four weeks and make a real difference.

English immersion (30 minutes a day)

  • Listen to Canadian English every day: CBC Radio, CTV News or Canadian podcasts. It tunes your ear to the voices and pace of everyday Canadian English.
  • Read one news article a day from a Canadian source. Skim for the main idea, then scan for details.
  • Think in English. While you commute, cook or wait, describe what you are doing in English. It builds the quick, unscripted speaking the Speaking section needs.

Vocabulary (10 minutes a day)

  • Learn five new words or phrases a day with the CELTESTPIP vocabulary builder or a flashcard app.
  • Focus on linking words (however, furthermore, in contrast, as a result). They help both Writing and Speaking.
  • Focus on opinion phrases (I strongly believe, in my view, the evidence suggests, it could be argued). You will need them in Speaking Tasks 5, 6 and 7.
  • Do not just memorise definitions. Use each new word in a sentence. For the full list, see CELPIP vocabulary: 100+ words and phrases.

Speaking practice (10 minutes a day)

  • Even on days focused on other skills, speak English out loud for 10 minutes.
  • Describe your day, summarise a news article, or explain a decision you made.
  • It keeps your speaking warm and cuts filler-word habits.

How to Adjust This CELPIP Study Plan for Your English Level

If you are already at CLB 7 or 8 and aiming for 9+

  • Squeeze Week 1 into two or three days, since you already know the format.
  • Spend more time on advanced strategies: tracking viewpoints, precise vocabulary in Writing, natural pacing in Speaking.
  • Focus your practice on Listening Parts 4 to 6 and Reading Part 4. That is where strong test takers gain or lose their edge.

If you are at CLB 5 or 6 and need CLB 7

  • Stretch the plan to six to eight weeks by repeating Weeks 2 and 3.
  • Spend 15 to 20 minutes a day on vocabulary instead of 10.
  • Focus on accuracy in Writing. Common errors at this level are verb tenses, articles and subject-verb agreement.
  • Do more Speaking: 20 minutes a day, aiming to fill the full response time.

If you are below CLB 5

  • Plan for 12 weeks or more. Spend the first weeks on everyday English (listening, reading and short daily writing), then start this plan at Week 1.
  • Give your weakest skill a short session every single day, not just on deep dive days.

If English is not the main language in your home

  • Raise your daily English immersion from 30 minutes to an hour.
  • Watch English shows or films with English subtitles, not subtitles in your first language.
  • Find a study partner or conversation group and speak regularly.
  • Write a short daily journal entry in English. Even 100 words helps.

What if you have less than four weeks?

Then this plan is too long, and stretching it thin helps nobody. Use the last-minute CELPIP study plan instead: it is built for one or two weeks, or just a few days, and it puts timed practice first.

Good to know

Frequently Asked Questions

1

Is 4 weeks (one month) enough to prepare for CELPIP?

Yes, for most test takers at an intermediate level (around CLB 6 to 7) who study 10 to 15 hours a week consistently. Below CLB 5, plan for 12 weeks or more. At CLB 8 or above, two to three weeks of focused practice is often enough.

2

How many hours per week should I study for CELPIP?

Plan for 10 to 15 hours a week: one to two hours on weekdays and two to three hours at weekends. Consistency beats long sessions. Ninety minutes a day does more than eight hours crammed into Saturday.

3

What's the best way to study for CELPIP?

Learn the format first, take a cold baseline, then spend most of your time on timed practice in your weakest skill, with a full mock test every week or two. Review every mistake and write down the fix. People who only do test after test without reviewing tend to plateau.

4

Should I focus on my weakest CELPIP section or practise all four?

Both. Follow the plan, which covers all four sections, but give extra time to your weakest one on the deep dive days. Most programs set a minimum in every skill, so one weak section can hold back your whole result.

5

When should I book my CELPIP test date?

Book it before you start studying. A fixed date gives you a deadline and stops preparation from drifting on for months. Use the table at the top of this page to pick a date you can realistically be ready for, and check available dates early so you get the centre you want.

6

What if my CELPIP practice scores aren't improving?

Switch from doing more practice tests to analyzing your mistakes. For every wrong answer, write down the correct answer, why you chose the wrong one, and what you will do differently next time. Patterns appear quickly, like misreading viewpoint questions or losing focus in long clips. Targeted fixes beat general practice every time.

7

Can I prepare for CELPIP for free?

Yes, a long way. CELPIP's free online sample test, every lesson in the CELTESTPIP courses, and the first question in every CELTESTPIP part and task (after a free sign-up: unlimited tries in Reading and Listening, up to three AI-scored tries per Writing and Speaking task) cover all four skills. Pro adds every other question and the full timed mock exams.

8

Can I use this study plan alongside a CELPIP preparation course?

Yes. Use the course for structured learning and teacher feedback, and use this plan for daily practice and self-study. Adjust the daily tasks to what your course covers so you do not do the same exercises twice.
