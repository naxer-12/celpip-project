Overview

Listening to Viewpoints is the final part of the CELPIP Listening Test. In this section, you will listen to a monologue where a single speaker discusses a specific topic, presenting various viewpoints, opinions, or arguments. Your task is to understand the speaker's perspectives, the reasoning behind these viewpoints, and any conclusions drawn.

Structure

  • Number of Questions: 6 questions
  • Audio Length: Approximately 3 minutes
  • Question Format: Multiple choice, with a set amount of time to answer all questions on the screen

What to Expect

  • Context: The speaker will usually be discussing a formal or semi-formal topic, such as social issues, technological advancements, or environmental concerns.
  • Focus: The monologue will involve the speaker presenting different viewpoints on the topic, often weighing the pros and cons or analyzing various aspects.
  • Content: The questions will typically cover the speaker's main argument, specific details supporting their viewpoints, any comparisons made, and the conclusions or opinions they express.

Key Features

  • One Speaker: The entire audio clip is a monologue by a single speaker.
  • Formal Tone: The speech is formal, often structured like a presentation or a well-prepared argument. The language may include specialized vocabulary or complex sentence structures.
  • Conceptual Content: The speaker may discuss abstract ideas, concepts, or proposals, requiring careful listening to understand the nuances.

Strategies and Tips

1. Set Up Your Notes Before Listening

  • Organize your notes to capture the main points, supporting details, and any contrasting viewpoints discussed by the speaker. This will help you keep track of the structure of the monologue.

2. Identify the Main Issue

  • Early in the monologue, the speaker will introduce the main issue or topic under discussion. Pay close attention to this introduction, as it sets the stage for the viewpoints that follow.

3. Listen for Viewpoints and Opinions

  • The speaker will likely present different viewpoints or opinions about the main issue. These could be their own views or those of others. Note these viewpoints and the reasons or evidence provided to support them.

4. Focus on Key Arguments

  • Identify the key arguments that the speaker emphasizes. These are often repeated or elaborated upon and are likely to be the focus of the questions.

5. Note Comparisons and Contrasts

  • If the speaker compares different viewpoints or contrasts opposing ideas, these comparisons are important. Questions may ask you to identify the differences or similarities between the viewpoints discussed.

6. Understand the Conclusion

  • Pay attention to how the speaker concludes their discussion. Do they favor one viewpoint over others? Do they offer a solution or recommendation? The conclusion often reflects the speaker's overall stance and is crucial for answering the final questions.

7. Recognize the Tone and Attitude

  • The speaker's tone can give you clues about their attitude towards the topic or the viewpoints they discuss. Is the tone supportive, critical, skeptical, or neutral? Understanding the tone can help in answering inference questions.

8. Paraphrasing Skills

  • The questions will often paraphrase the information presented in the monologue. Be prepared to identify the same idea expressed in different words. This skill is critical for accurately selecting the correct answer.

9. Manage Your Time

  • Since all questions for this part are presented together on one screen, allocate your time wisely. Answer the questions you are confident about first, then return to the more challenging ones.

Common Pitfalls

  • Missing the Main Idea: Ensure you understand the main issue or topic before focusing on the details. Misunderstanding the main idea can lead to errors in answering subsequent questions.
  • Overlooking Details: While focusing on the main arguments, do not neglect the supporting details, as these are often the subject of specific questions.
  • Confusing Viewpoints: Clearly differentiate between the different viewpoints presented. Mixing up these viewpoints can lead to incorrect answers.

Final Advice

Listening to Viewpoints tests your ability to comprehend complex discussions and multiple perspectives. To succeed in this section, practice listening to monologues on various topics and focus on identifying and understanding different viewpoints, key arguments, and the speaker's overall stance. With adequate preparation, you’ll be able to navigate this challenging part of the CELPIP Listening Test effectively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Listening Part 6.

Is CELPIP Listening Part 6 the hardest section?

CELPIP Listening Part 6 (Viewpoints) is the final and one of the hardest Listening sections: a roughly 3-minute formal monologue presenting several viewpoints on an abstract topic, which you must keep separate while tracking the speaker's conclusion.

What is CELPIP Listening Part 6 about?

CELPIP Listening Part 6 is a single speaker presenting multiple viewpoints on a formal topic (social issues, technology, or the environment) weighing pros and cons before reaching a conclusion. It is the only Listening part that is a long, structured monologue.

How do I follow the different viewpoints in CELPIP Listening Part 6?

In CELPIP Listening Part 6, catch the main issue early, then note each viewpoint with its reasons and any comparisons the speaker makes. Pay close attention to the conclusion, whether the speaker favours one view or gives a recommendation, since it is often tested.

How long is CELPIP Listening Part 6 and how many questions?

CELPIP Listening Part 6 is about 3 minutes of continuous audio with 6 questions, shown together on one screen. The formal language and abstract ideas make organized note-taking essential, and answers are usually paraphrased rather than quoted.

Can I take notes in CELPIP Listening Part 6?

Yes, and you should, because CELPIP Listening Part 6's 3-minute monologue is dense with viewpoints. Jot each viewpoint, its supporting reasons, and the conclusion so you are not relying on memory when the questions appear.

What kind of questions are in CELPIP Listening Part 6?

CELPIP Listening Part 6 asks about the main idea, each viewpoint's supporting details, comparisons the speaker makes, and the final conclusion, usually paraphrased. Missing the main idea early is the most common reason for lost marks.

How do I improve at CELPIP Listening Part 6?

Because CELPIP Listening Part 6 is the toughest listening section, practice with longer, formal monologues and lectures, noting each viewpoint and the conclusion. Building academic vocabulary and the stamina for 3 minutes of dense speech makes the biggest difference.
