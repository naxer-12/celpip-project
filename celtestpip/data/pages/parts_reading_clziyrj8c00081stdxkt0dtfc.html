What is CELPIP Reading Part 1 (Reading Correspondence)?

Reading Part 1, also called Reading Task 1, gives you an email from one person to another about an everyday social or work situation, then a short reply with blanks. You answer 11 questions in about 11 minutes. It is the first and easiest of the four Reading parts, and it sets your pace for the rest.

How many questions are in Reading Part 1, and how long do you get?

Part 1 has 11 questions in two sets: 6 about the email, then 5 that complete the reply. CELPIP gives about 11 minutes for the whole part.

Each Reading part runs on its own clock. You can change your answers as often as you like while the time lasts. When it runs out, the test moves to the next part on its own and you cannot go back.

Every correct answer is worth 1 point and a wrong answer costs nothing, so never leave a question blank. Part 1 counts toward one CELPIP Reading level, based on all 38 scored questions. The CELPIP Reading score chart shows how many correct answers each level needs.

What will you read in Reading Part 1?

The main text is an email between people who deal with each other in daily life. CELPIP's own examples include baseball team members discussing a competition, a customer asking a bakery for a graduation cake, someone emailing an animal shelter about volunteering, and colleagues planning travel for a conference.

The tone is conversational and friendly, but never extreme: no slang and no expressions of love. That matters in the reply, because the right answer has to fit the relationship between the two writers. If the name of this part still feels unclear, read what Reading Correspondence means in CELPIP.

How should you spend the 11 minutes?

CELPIP publishes a suggested plan for this part in its Reading study pack. It adds up to 11 minutes:

  1. Preview the email (30 seconds). Find the writer, the recipient, the purpose, and whether the writer asks for something.
  2. Read question set 1 (1.5 minutes). Mark the keywords in each question before you go back to the email.
  3. Answer question set 1 (4 minutes). Use the keywords to find the right part of the email. Read closely or skim, depending on how much text there is, and watch for paraphrase.
  4. Answer question set 2 (4.5 minutes). Complete the reply. Each choice has to agree with the first email and suit the tone between the two writers.
  5. Review (30 seconds). Make sure every question has an answer.

For a pacing plan that covers all four parts, see our CELPIP Reading time guide.

Tips for CELPIP Reading Part 1

  • Know why the email was written. Is the writer asking, informing, or answering an earlier message? Then ask what the reader is expected to do next. Most general meaning questions turn on these two points.
  • Answer from the line, not from memory. Names, dates, places and requests carry the specific information questions. Go back to the sentence before you choose.
  • Expect paraphrase. The correct option almost never repeats the email's words. An option that copies a phrase but changes one detail is usually the trap.
  • Read the reply's tone. In set 2, two options can both be good English. Pick the one that fits the meaning and the relationship, not the one that sounds most formal.

The video above shows these techniques on a real Part 1 question. For more, read the Reading Part 1 tips in our Reading guide or take the free Reading Correspondence lesson.

Common mistakes in Reading Part 1

  • Missing a small detail, such as a changed date or a second request near the end of the email.
  • Choosing a reply word that is correct English but wrong for the tone.
  • Taking an option that looks right on its own but does not fit the whole message.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about Reading Part 1.

How do the fill-in-the-blank questions work in CELPIP Reading Part 1?

The second text is a short reply to the first email, with blanks. For each blank you choose from four options. The right one agrees with what the first email said and fits the tone between the two writers. Wrong options are often good English that does not match the context.

What kinds of emails appear in CELPIP Reading Part 1?

Everyday social and work emails between people who deal with each other. CELPIP's examples include team members discussing a competition, a customer asking a bakery for a cake, someone asking an animal shelter about volunteering, and colleagues arranging conference travel. The tone is friendly and conversational, with no slang.

How much time do I have per question in CELPIP Reading Part 1?

About 11 minutes for 11 questions, so roughly one minute each. CELPIP's suggested plan is 30 seconds to preview the email, 1.5 minutes to read the first question set, 4 minutes to answer it, 4.5 minutes for the reply, and 30 seconds to check that every question has an answer.

Why do the answer choices in CELPIP Reading Part 1 look so similar?

Because the correct option paraphrases the email instead of repeating its words. Focus on meaning, not on matching vocabulary. Be careful with an option that copies a phrase from the text but changes one detail: that is usually the wrong answer.

Does CELPIP Reading Part 1 have its own score?

No. Part 1 counts toward one CELPIP Reading level, based on all 38 scored Reading questions. Each correct answer is worth 1 point and a wrong answer costs nothing, so answer every question, even when you have to guess.

How can I improve at CELPIP Reading Part 1?

Practise with everyday emails and ask two questions each time: why was this written, and what does the writer want? Learn to spot paraphrase, and match the reply's tone to the relationship between the writers. Then do timed sets until 11 minutes feels comfortable.

Can I change my answers in CELPIP Reading Part 1?

Yes, as many times as you like until the time for Part 1 runs out. Each Reading part has its own time. When it ends, the test moves to Part 2 on its own and you cannot go back, so check your answers before the clock reaches zero.
