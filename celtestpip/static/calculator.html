IRCC turns each skill on your test into its own CLB level. CELPIP-General matches one to one: CELPIP 7 is CLB 7. On IELTS General Training, CLB 7 is 6.0 in every skill, and CLB 9 is 8.0 Listening and 7.0 in the rest. On PTE Core, CLB 7 starts at 60 Listening and Reading, 68 Speaking and 69 Writing.

Use the calculator above to see your CLB in each skill and your Express Entry language points, or read the full tables further down. They come from IRCC and use the same skill order every time.

What is a CLB level, and is it one number or four?

Four. CLB stands for Canadian Language Benchmark, the scale Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) uses to read every English test it accepts. Your Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking results each get their own CLB level. There is no overall CLB.

That changes how you read your result. Each program sets a minimum for each skill. The Federal Skilled Worker Program asks for CLB 7 in all four, and the Federal Skilled Trades Program asks for CLB 5 in Speaking and Listening and CLB 4 in Reading and Writing (IRCC, Express Entry language test results). One weak skill can sink an application even when the other three are strong.

For what each program needs and how many CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) points every level earns, see our guide to CLB minimums and CRS points for Express Entry.

How do IELTS General Training scores convert to CLB?

Find your band in each column, then read the CLB level on the left. You need at least the score in a row to reach that level. IELTS Listening 7.0, for example, clears the CLB 7 row (6.0) but not the CLB 8 row (7.5), so it counts as CLB 7.

How do TEF Canada and TCF Canada scores convert to NCLC?

The same way, on NCLC, the French version of the scale. Express Entry counts an NCLC level exactly like the same CLB level, so NCLC 9 earns the same points as CLB 9.

What is CLB 9 on IELTS, PTE Core and CELPIP?

CLB 9 is where IELTS stops being even. You need 8.0 in Listening but 7.0 in the other three.

Which English tests does IRCC accept for Express Entry?

Three: CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training and PTE Core. IRCC lists them on its Express Entry language test page, and your results must be less than 2 years old both when you create your profile and when you apply.

French tests (TEF Canada and TCF Canada) use NCLC, the French version of the scale, and IRCC publishes their tables on the same page.

Applying for something other than Express Entry? Minimums differ. See the CELPIP score you need for citizenship or CELPIP for a post-graduation work permit.

Why does the calculator show two levels?

Because CELPIP's own chart does. In Listening and Reading, celpip.ca publishes approximate ranges of correct answers for each level, and the ranges overlap. A Listening score of 33 sits in both the level 8 range (30 to 33) and the level 9 range (33 to 35), because questions differ in difficulty from one test to another, so the number of correct answers a level needs can shift slightly. When you see "8 or 9", plan with the lower number and aim a few points higher in your next practice test.

Frequently asked questions

Is CLB the same as CELPIP?

No, but on CELPIP they share numbers. CLB is IRCC's scale for every accepted test. CELPIP is one test. A CELPIP level of 3 or higher is the same number on the CLB scale.

Is my CLB level my lowest score?

No. You have four CLB levels, one per skill. Programs set a minimum for each skill, so your lowest skill decides whether you meet a minimum, but each skill keeps its own level.

Is there a CLB calculator?

Yes, at the top of this page. Pick your test, enter your four scores and it shows your CLB in each skill. The tables below give the same result by hand.

Does CELPIP-General LS convert to CLB the same way?

Yes. CELPIP-General LS reports Listening and Speaking only, on the same level scale, so each level maps to the same CLB. Express Entry does not accept it, though: IRCC asks for the full CELPIP-General. The LS test is used for Canadian citizenship, which needs CLB 4 in Listening and Speaking.

Where do these numbers come from?

From IRCC. Every table on this page is copied from canada.ca, linked under each table. If IRCC changes a table, its page is the one to trust, and we'll update ours.
