What does a CELPIP practice test look like?

Here is one real question from a full mock test so you can see the format before you start. This is a Reading for Information item, the kind that scores instantly.

Passage extract: A community centre notice states that the pottery studio will be closed for renovations from March 3 to March 17. Registered members can use the partner studio on Elm Street at no extra cost during that period.


Question: What can registered members do while the pottery studio is closed?

A. Get a refund for the closed period

B. Use the Elm Street studio for free C. Book private renovation tours D. Nothing until March 17


Answer: B. The notice says members "can use the partner studio on Elm Street at no extra cost," so free access to the Elm Street studio is the only option the text supports. A, C, and D are never stated, and CELPIP Reading rewards the answer the passage actually backs up, not the one that sounds reasonable.

Listening and Reading items like this are multiple choice and score the moment you finish. Writing and Speaking work differently: you submit your response and CELTESTPIP returns an AI CLB estimate within seconds, with feedback on each scoring criterion so you know exactly where you lost marks instead of guessing.

How is a CELPIP mock test scored, and what CLB do I need?

CELPIP reports each of the four skills as a level from 0 to 12, and from level 3 up that level is your Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB), the national standard IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) uses to read your results. Listening and Reading are scored right or wrong. Writing and Speaking are judged on four criteria: Content and Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability or Listenability, and Task Fulfillment.

What score you actually need depends on why you are taking the test.

Your goalMinimum CLBSource
Federal Skilled Worker, first official languageCLB 7 in each skillIRCC Express Entry
Maximum CRS language points, Express EntryCLB 10 or higher in each skillIRCC Express Entry
Canadian citizenship, ages 18 to 54CLB 4 in Speaking and ListeningIRCC citizenship

CLB 7 in all four abilities is the Federal Skilled Worker floor and also covers the Canadian Experience Class for TEER 0 or 1 jobs, per the IRCC Express Entry language test rules. Push to CLB 9 in every skill and your CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) language points jump, with the most at CLB 10 or higher. That jump is often the difference between an invitation and another six months in the pool. For citizenship, you only need CLB 4 in Speaking and Listening if you are 18 to 54, and there is no Reading or Writing requirement, per the IRCC citizenship language proof rules.

This is a summary, not the full picture. For the exact raw-score to CLB bands by skill, and how the numbers convert, see CELPIP scores for Canadian immigration and the Reading score chart.

How should I use mock tests to prepare?

Start with one full mock test under real timed conditions before you do anything else, so you get an honest baseline in each skill instead of a hopeful guess. Then work section by section. For Listening and Reading, read the explanation for every question you missed and look for the pattern: is it always the viewpoint questions, always the inference, always the ones where you ran out of time? For Writing and Speaking, read the AI feedback criterion by criterion and fix the one costing you the most marks first.

Drill your weakest sections with focused practice, then come back and take another full mock. Do at least two or three complete three-hour tests before your real test date. This is as much about stamina as skill. The candidates who crash are usually the ones who practiced in 20-minute bursts and then hit a wall at hour two of the real exam.

Can I pause and resume a CELPIP practice test?

Yes, you can pause a practice test and resume it later, and every section runs on a real exam timer so you build pacing. That said, do at least some of your mocks in one uninterrupted sitting. The real CELPIP test runs about three hours with no breaks, and the only way to train for that is to actually do it once or twice before test day, timer running, phone away.

Start a full mock exam when you are ready for the full three-hour rehearsal, or keep building with free CELPIP questions first.
