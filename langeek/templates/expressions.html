Idioms
Everyday Idiom /po.ze œ̃ la.pɛ̃/

Poser un lapin

Literal: To put a rabbit
Meaning: To stand someone up (fail to show up for a date/meeting)
Exemple: Je l'ai attendu une heure au café, mais il m'a posé un lapin.
I waited an hour for him at the café, but he stood me up.
Romantic & Cultural /ku də fudʁ/

Coup de foudre

Literal: A bolt of lightning
Meaning: Love at first sight
Exemple: Dès qu'ils se sont vus à Paris, ce fut le coup de foudre.
As soon as they saw each other in Paris, it was love at first sight.
Feelings & Mood /a.vwaʁ lə ka.faʁ/

Avoir le cafard

Literal: To have the cockroach
Meaning: To feel down, melancholy, or have the blues
Exemple: Quand il pleut toute la journée, j'ai souvent le cafard.
When it rains all day, I often feel down.
Honesty & Frankness /ap.le œ̃ ʃa œ̃ ʃa/

Appeler un chat un chat

Literal: To call a cat a cat
Meaning: To call a spade a spade / speak bluntly and plainly
Exemple: Soyons directs et appelons un chat un chat.
Let's be direct and call a spade a spade.
